\chapter{Discussion: Reproducibility Across Implementations}
\label{chap:reproducibility-discussion}

None of the validation checks in Chapter~\ref{chap:mhd-validation-results}
found the implementation failures they target, and none measures physical
accuracy. A matched difference that clears them is therefore a response to the
declared axis with its mechanism left open, which is what makes the
comparisons below interpretable without turning every discrepancy into error.

\section{Relative importance of the tested axes}
\label{sec:ch6-relative-importance}

Importance is case- and criterion-specific, and a small domain average can hide
a large local one: Orszag--Tang/HLLD at $512^2$ gave
$L_\infty=5.031\times10^{-2}$ against a mean-$L_1$ of $5.554\times10^{-5}$. The
obvious reading, that shocks and current sheets occupy few cells so displacing
one dominates $L_\infty$, holds for HLLD but not generally: the mapped
discrepancy of Section~\ref{sec:ch5-cross-system} puts $38\%$ of HLLD's total
on $1\%$ of cells and only $6\%$ of HLL's, against $1\%$ for a uniform field.
Localisation is a solver property, and the diffusive clamped HLL path spreads
its discrepancy much as it spreads the features themselves.

HLL replaces the internal Riemann fan by a two-wave approximation that the GLM
clamp reduces further to a globally scaled Rusanov flux
(Section~\ref{sec:ch2-riemann}), whereas HLLD restores the intermediate waves
\citep{miyoshi_kusano_2005} and carries 14 guarded degeneracy decisions. Its
non-zero branch response must originate at the intermediate ties, since HLL's
own equality branches are unreachable by construction rather than by luck
(Section~\ref{sec:ch5-build-sensitivity}).
Instrumenting those decisions makes the argument a measurement rather than an
inference, and narrows it. Of the three ties only $S_M$ is ever exactly zero on
Brio--Wu, and it is zero at $62\%$ of interface evaluations, while
Orszag--Tang reaches no exact tie at all; the fourteen degeneracy guards never
fire on either. At a matched time step HLLD also attains the higher observed
refinement rate on both two-dimensional cases
(Section~\ref{sec:ch4-orszag-tang}), so the sensitivity is not bought by
inaccuracy. The lesson is one of counting rather than quality: exposure to
an implementation detail follows from how many internal decisions are actually
reached, not from accuracy or dissipation, so the more accurate Riemann solver
is here the more implementation-sensitive one, and it is sensitive through one
specific decision rather than through its elaborateness in general.

The null CPU/GPU saved-state differences establish deterministic equivalence for
those matched configurations, but that equivalence was arranged rather than
found: it needs device contraction disabled to match the host, and restoring
nvcc's own default removed it in every pair (Section~\ref{sec:ch5-hardware}).
Bitwise agreement across processor types is therefore a property of the build
configuration, not of the hardware. Nor does the larger difference make the
contracted result the worse one, since a fused multiply--add rounds once where
the host rounds twice; the axis is ranked by displacement from the baseline,
not by accuracy, which is also why the still more relaxed
\texttt{--use\_fast\_math} build sits closer to the host. The instruction-set
null result sharpens the point: widening the registers changed nothing, because
the semantics rather than the instruction mix decide.

These distinctions stop ``stable'' becoming shorthand for ``reproducible'':
completion, a changed saved field and an identical stored representation are
three different claims.

The temporal study is partly a statement about sampling. Both cases approach a
common level near 2.3 roundings in the density mean at the resolutions used
(Section~\ref{sec:ch5-temporal}), so the expectation that a chaotic
two-dimensional problem must separate faster than a one-dimensional shock tube
is not tested here: the series differ in sampling window, not in mechanism. It
is also a statement about mechanism. Every axis followed in time, precision and
both build axes, is better described by a power law than by an exponential,
with exponents near 1.4--1.6. Growth is therefore governed by how many
operations have been performed rather than by which axis introduced the
difference, and the e-folding reading of a fitted $\lambda$ does not apply.

Performance is workload-dependent: in cell updates per second the CPU rate
barely moved between the two problems while the GPU rate spanned seventyfold,
which is what amortising fixed launch, transfer and output costs looks like.
Every CPU rate here is single-core, so the device is compared against one core
rather than the machine, and the crossover would move with a work-shared host.
Modest precision speed-ups likewise need not track arithmetic throughput when
memory traffic and control flow share the runtime. That reading suits a
bandwidth-bound placement on the Roofline model
\citep{williamsEtAl2009roofline}, but no operational intensity or hardware
counter was measured, so the placement is inferred rather than established.

\section{Reproducibility of a published algorithm}
\label{sec:ch6-published-algorithm}

Operationally the MUSCL--Hancock/GLM update proved reliable: across two
precisions, four CFL numbers, three grids and two devices, every reported run
finished with finite, positive states, the one Orszag--Tang HLLD exception
needing a fallback that then fired twice in three million line updates.
Insensitivity is another matter, since relaxed floating-point semantics and the
HLLD branch rule each moved the saved field, and the fp32--fp64 separation grew
under refinement instead of shrinking. A scheme can be perfectly stable and
still fail to reproduce bit for bit across builds.

Reproducing a published algorithm therefore requires more than naming HLL,
HLLD or MUSCL--Hancock. The record must fix an immutable source revision, a
build recipe with the effective compiler and floating-point options, a complete
case definition, the algorithmic choices that alter the arithmetic path, and
the execution platform, acceptance criteria and timing protocol actually used,
bound to the retained artefacts by hash
\citep{plesser2018reproducibility,sandveEtAl2013reproducible};
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-provenance} supplies them. Containers preserve much
of the software environment, but not hardware-dependent arithmetic.

The record supports two distinct claims. Bitwise reproducibility requires the
same stored representation under tightly matched execution; scientific
reproducibility requires the declared conclusion to survive comparison with a
justified reference and stated tolerances, and can stand where bitwise
agreement between independent implementations is infeasible, provided
unexplained differences are exposed through retained outputs.
