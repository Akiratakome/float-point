\chapter{MHD Verification and Case-Specific Validation Results}
\label{chap:mhd-validation-results}

Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology} defines the reference hierarchy and the
diagnostics used here; each result below is conditional on its solver,
precision, device, grid and final time.

\section{Order verification and the fp32 floor}
\label{sec:ch4-cp-alfven}

The circularly polarised Alfv\'en wave is the one case admitting an observed
order of accuracy: it is an exact nonlinear solution at finite amplitude, since
$|\mathbf B|$ and the total pressure stay uniform. With $\rho=1$, $p=0.1$,
$B_x=1$, $\mathbf B_\perp=A(\sin2\pi x,\cos2\pi x)$ at $A=0.1$ and
$\mathbf v_\perp=-\mathbf B_\perp/\sqrt{\rho}$, the Alfv\'en speed is unity, so
the state at $t=1$ on the unit periodic domain is the initial condition
translated through one wavelength, that is, itself
\citep{toth_2000,gardinerStone2005ct}, and the final-minus-analytic difference
is true discretisation error. The data are $y$-independent and exactly
solenoidal by construction, with $\mathrm{divB}_{\max}$ below
$1.2\times10^{-13}$ on every grid, so GLM cleaning does not enter.

Refining from $N=32$ to $N=512$, the transverse field mean $L_1$ error falls
from $6.565\times10^{-3}$ to $4.288\times10^{-5}$, a fitted order of $1.818$
with $R^2=0.9997$. The pairwise orders rise monotonically, $1.706$, $1.822$,
$1.844$, $1.886$, approaching second order from below as the limiter's
influence near the extrema of a smooth wave recedes. Over this range the fp32
and fp64 errors differ by at most $0.3\%$ and the CPU and GPU groups are
bitwise identical, so the same order statement covers the CUDA HLL path by
inheritance rather than as independent device evidence.

Because the error is measured against an exact field rather than a finer
calculation, extending the ladder locates the precision floor directly.
Continued to $N=8192$ (Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-cp-alfven}), fp64 keeps converging
and its pairwise order reaches $2.08$ on the finest pair, whereas fp32 leaves
the fp64 curve at $N=2048$: its excess over the matched fp64 error grows from
$0.3\%$ at $N=512$ through $13\%$ to $421\%$ at $N=8192$, and its pairwise order
breaks from the fp64 sequence, so past that grid the fp32 slope records
accumulated rounding rather than an order of accuracy. As a budget, fp64 at
$N=4096$ reaches $7.438\times10^{-7}$, below the $9.149\times10^{-7}$ fp32
attains at $N=8192$: refinement in fp32 loses to fp64 on half as many cells.
This is the report's only accuracy statement resting on an exact field.

\section{Brio--Wu numerical-reference assessment}
\label{sec:ch4-brio-wu}

Three fp64 grids of the coplanar MHD Riemann problem of \citet{brio_wu_1988}
were compared against an $N=8000$ numerical comparator, conservatively
downsampled by averaging the $8000/N$ fine cells in each candidate cell. From
$N=200$ to $N=800$ the density mean $L_1$ difference fell from
$1.481\times10^{-2}$ to $5.642\times10^{-3}$ and $L_2$ from
$3.642\times10^{-2}$ to $1.923\times10^{-2}$, with
$\mathrm{divB}_{\max}\leq4.441\times10^{-14}$; least-squares fits of
$\log_2 E=a-q\log_2 N$ give $q=0.70$ in $L_1$ and $0.46$ in $L_2$. Since the
order is verified separately (Section~\ref{sec:ch4-cp-alfven}), these rates
measure what discontinuities cost it. The standard smearing estimate for a
linearly degenerate contact, at which an order-$p$ monotone scheme converges in
$L_1$ as $h^{p/(p+1)}$ \citep{banksEtAl2008sublinear,leveque_2002}, gives
$h^{2/3}$ for $p=2$, close to the observed $0.70$; the compound wave, whose
limit under refinement is scheme-dependent rather than unique, and the
comparator's shared discretisation bias both admit pseudo-convergence
\citep{torrilhon2003pseudo}.

The $N=800$ profiles in Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-brio-profiles} reproduce the
sequence of \citet{brio_wu_1988} --- fast rarefaction, slow compound wave,
contact, slow shock, fast rarefaction --- with the contact and slow shock
displaced and smeared against the comparator, where the mean $L_1$ difference
concentrates.

\section{Two-dimensional property verification and divergence control}
\label{sec:ch4-invariance-glm}

An $800\times4$ embedding of Brio--Wu preserved a transversely constant state
exactly, every row identical component by component, settling invariance
directly. Against the one-dimensional run the mean and maximum absolute density
differences were $3.550\times10^{-4}$ and $7.034\times10^{-3}$, below the
predeclared $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}$, but the $y$-direction fast speed forces 861
steps against 759, so that residual mixes temporal discretisation with any
transverse diffusion.

Cleaning was tested on a state non-solenoidal by construction: a Gaussian
normal-field bump $B_x=B_0\exp(-r^2/\sigma^2)$ at $(0.5,0.5)$ on a
doubly-periodic $128^2$ square, for which divergence is of order unity. At
$t=0.5$, $\max|\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{B}|$ was 3.030 undamped, 0.2678 at
$c_r=0.18$ and 0.8429 at $c_r=0.36$, one HLL fp64 run per setting at CFL 0.4.
Since the damping rate is $c_h/c_r$ the chosen value damps the more strongly,
though three settings at one run each order the values rather than resolve the
dependence.

\section{Matched CPU/GPU implementation verification}
\label{sec:ch4-cpu-gpu-validation}

Case, grid, precision, arithmetic and update order were matched across separate
CPU and CUDA HLL implementations. Table~\ref{tab:ch4-cpu-gpu} reports four
pairs, Brio--Wu at $800\times1$ and Orszag--Tang at $256^2$ in both precisions,
each repeated five times, all giving zero maximum ULP and zero absolute
$L_\infty$. The $c_h$ reduction was reordered into a parallel GPU maximum tree,
which cannot accumulate rounding, and device contraction was disabled to match
the host (Section~\ref{sec:ch2-execution}), so the agreement holds for that
build recipe, and a shared defect would agree bit for bit too.

\section{Orszag--Tang validation}
\label{sec:ch4-orszag-tang}

The HLL runs at $128^2$, $256^2$ and $512^2$ completed with finite, positive
states. The divergence diagnostic is dimensional, so the ladder is read in grid
units: over the three grids the cell-width-scaled domain mean falls
$1.07\times10^{-3}$, $4.79\times10^{-4}$, $2.02\times10^{-4}$, about fivefold,
so cleaning works wherever the field is smooth, while the maximum moves only
from 0.0136 to 0.0145 to 0.0151, staying near $1.4\%$ of $B_0$ per cell width.
Without explicit resistivity ideal MHD current sheets keep thinning under
refinement, leaving a sheet a few cells across whose central-difference residual
scales as $1/h$, so the peak stays pinned at the grid scale and its raw growth
reflects the diagnostic's dimensions rather than lost divergence control. The
fp64 HLL $256^2$--$512^2$ pair retained zero relative mass drift, and its
temperature field preserved the interacting-shock and current-sheet topology of
published calculations \citep{toth_2000,bard_dorelli_2014}
(Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-ot-morphology}).

Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-resolution-precision} gives the adjacent-grid diagnostic
$p_{\mathrm{obs}}$ of Section~\ref{sec:ch3-metrics}. The two-dimensional update
composes its sweeps as a fixed $x$-then-$y$ Lie splitting, first order as a
multidimensional split (Section~\ref{sec:ch2-mhd-glm}), so the reference is
$p\simeq1$ rather than 2, and minmod limiting lowers it wherever it activates.
For fp64 HLL the differences were 0.1203 and 0.07722, giving $p\simeq0.639$, and
for HLLD \citep{miyoshi_kusano_2005} 0.07515 and 0.04182, giving
$p\simeq0.846$; the fp32 values agreed at the displayed precision. Both fall
below it, as an active limiter at shocks and current sheets would produce, and
for HLL the globally clamped wave speed adds Rusanov dissipation everywhere on
top.

Because the two ladders ran at different time steps, HLL was re-run at the HLLD
setting. At CFL 0.2 it gives $p\simeq0.640$ for Orszag--Tang and $0.917$ for
Kelvin--Helmholtz, against $0.639$ and $0.919$ at CFL 0.4, so the rates are
insensitive to the time step. At that matched step HLLD attains the higher rate
on both cases, $0.846$ and $1.442$, so the ordering is a solver comparison,
consistent with the clamped HLL flux carrying extra dissipation.

An exploratory fp64 HLLD run at $512^2$ terminated with diagnosed negative
pressure at CFL 0.4 and again at 0.2, so the time step was not the fix. Pressure
is recovered as
$p=(\gamma-1)[E-\rho|\mathbf{v}|^2/2-|\mathbf{B}|^2/2]$, so where magnetic and
kinetic energy dominate $E$ the subtraction cancels leading digits and the
relative error in $p$ is amplified by roughly $E/p$ \citep{higham_2002}. This
recovery is the standard positivity weak point of an energy-conservative MHD
update \citep{miyoshi_kusano_2005}; the guard detects it, though whether its
threshold moves with the working precision was not tested. With the per-line
first-order HLL fallback enabled the ladder completed at all three grids, and it
carries almost none of the result: the recomputation fired twice in
$3\,355\,648$ line updates at $512^2$ and not at all at $256^2$ or on Brio--Wu,
while HLLD's fourteen degeneracy guards never fired in $1.7\times10^{9}$
interface evaluations, so no tested interface was degenerate and the guards
stand unexercised. Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-ot-solver-comparison} shows where the two
$256^2$ fields differ against the same HLL $512^2$ context.

\section{Kelvin--Helmholtz validation}
\label{sec:ch4-kelvin-helmholtz}

The initial data (Equation~\ref{eq:ch3-ic-kh}) are a smooth double shear layer
of width $a=0.025$, a single-wavelength $\sin(2\pi x)$ seed and a uniform
flow-aligned field at Alfv\'en Mach number 5. At equal densities an aligned
field suppresses the instability only for a velocity jump below $2v_A$
\citep{chandrasekhar1961stability}; here that jump is $1.0$ against
$2v_A=0.2$, so the case sits well inside the unstable regime, where the field
modifies the nonlinear stage without removing it \citep{frankEtAl1996kh}. At a
sonic Mach number near 0.39 the case is subsonic, so density is a low-contrast
diagnostic here. A
separate reproduction tested the linear stage directly. On the smooth
unstratified initial condition of \citet{lecoanetEtAl2016kh}, with
$\mathbf{B}=0$ so the solver reduces to inviscid hydrodynamics, the $k=2\pi$
mode amplitude of \citet{tricco2019kh} grew at rates rising 1.079, 2.193, 2.731
and 2.888 from $64\times128$ to $512\times1024$ against the published linear
value of 3.227 \citep{berlokPfrommer2019kh}: deficits of 67\%, 32\%, 15\% and
10\% that halve over the first three doublings and then flatten. The shortfall
is therefore under-resolution of the layer rather than a wrong mechanism, the
residual near 10\% being what the clamped HLL path's dissipation costs. The
check bounds linear hydrodynamic behaviour only, on a different initial
condition with no field.

All solver--precision groups completed at $128^2$, $256^2$ and $512^2$ with
finite, positive states. Scaled by cell width and $B_0=0.1$, both divergence
maxima fall monotonically at $t=1.0$, from $4.7\times10^{-5}$ to
$1.8\times10^{-5}$ for HLL and from $7.1\times10^{-4}$ to $1.0\times10^{-4}$ for
HLLD: two to three orders tighter than Orszag--Tang's $1.4\%$, and tightening
under refinement rather than pinned at the grid scale, since the shear layer
keeps a fixed width. HLLD's maximum stays about an order of magnitude above
HLL's, so low dissipation and tight discrete divergence control are not obtained
together.

The matched-step fp64 rates quoted in Section~\ref{sec:ch4-orszag-tang} read the
same way here: the HLL value sits close to the splitting-limited reference,
whereas the HLLD value exceeding one indicates three grids short of an
asymptotic regime rather than a better-than-first-order method.
Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-kh-morphology} shows the field these rates describe.
