\subsection{Euler--MHD cross-system sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-cross-system}

All sixteen Sod, LW3, Brio--Wu and Orszag--Tang runs in
Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-cross-system} completed. The O2-default fp32--fp64
mean-relative $L_1$ stays between two and seven roundings, so in the domain mean
the updates deliver about the unit roundoff of the working precision and do not
amplify it at these resolutions \citep{higham_2002}; amplification appears only
in the absolute $L_\infty$.

Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-localisation} maps $|\rho_{32}-\rho_{64}|$ for matched
Orszag--Tang pairs at $256^2$. For HLLD the difference is strongly localised on
the shock and current-sheet network, $L_\infty/\text{mean}=2304$ with the top
$1\%$ of cells carrying $38\%$ of the total; for HLL the ratio is 12.5 and the
top $1\%$ carry $6\%$. On this case concentration follows the solver rather than
the flow features alone.

\subsection{Effective build-semantics sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-build-sensitivity}

The build pair in Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-cross-system} compared O2-default with
Ofast-fast, which raises the optimisation level and relaxes floating-point
semantics together, confounding the two. In fp64 the
mean-relative density response lay between $4.24\times10^{-17}$ and
$4.21\times10^{-16}$; in fp32 it ranged from exactly zero for Sod to
$5.124\times10^{-7}$ for LW3, exceeding that case's $3.762\times10^{-7}$
fp32--fp64 difference.

At $N=800$, $t=0.1$, CFL 0.4 and one thread, a Microsoft Visual C++
(MSVC)~19.51 Brio--Wu rebuild varied each axis separately
(Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-build-semantics}). All four /Ox--/O2
discrepancies were zero, so optimisation level alone left the output unchanged.
By contrast /fp:fast against compiler-default math
\citep{microsoftMsvcFp2021} gave $L_\infty$ values of $1.887\times10^{-15}$ and
$1.311\times10^{-6}$ for HLL and $4.413\times10^{-15}$ and
$1.729\times10^{-6}$ for HLLD, in fp64 and fp32, the larger fp32 response
following its coarser unit roundoff. Changing $\leq$ to $<$ gave zero for both
HLL groups but $3.886\times10^{-15}$ and $2.608\times10^{-6}$ for HLLD. The HLL
zero is structural, since the clamp forces $S_L^{H}<0<S_R^{H}$ so neither outer
dispatch branch can be selected (Section~\ref{sec:ch2-riemann}), whereas HLLD
keeps the unclamped Davis speeds and three further ties at $S_L^*$, $S_R^*$ and
$S_M$, across each of which the flux is value-continuous: the variants diverge
only where rounding decides the side of an exact zero.

Moving only the baseline instruction set from /arch:SSE2 to /arch:AVX2 left all
four groups bitwise identical although the binaries differed by hash, since
/fp:precise forbids the reassociation wider vector registers would otherwise
invite.

\subsection{Matched CPU/GPU agreement and performance}
\label{sec:ch5-hardware}

The bit-identical CPU/GPU states of Table~\ref{tab:ch4-cpu-gpu} are reproduced
by the $n=5$ groups of Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-hardware}. Since one device flag
arranges them, that flag was varied: rebuilding only the CUDA kernel translation
unit with nvcc's default \texttt{--fmad=true}, holding host binary, case, grid,
precision and update order fixed, removed bitwise agreement in all four pairs.
The density $L_\infty$ differences were $1.665\times10^{-15}$ and
$2.309\times10^{-14}$ in fp64 for Brio--Wu and Orszag--Tang, and
$2.265\times10^{-6}$ and $2.074\times10^{-5}$ in fp32. The Brio--Wu fp32 value
shares case, grid, solver and final time with two axes already reported and
exceeds both, the $1.075\times10^{-6}$ fp32--fp64 difference and the
$1.311\times10^{-6}$ /fp:fast response: one device compiler default moved the
stored field at least as far as halving the working precision did, a comparison
Section~\ref{sec:ch5-mca} then bounds.

Contraction is only part of nvcc's relaxed-math setting, so the same kernels
were rebuilt with \texttt{--use\_fast\_math}, which also enables flush-to-zero,
approximate division and square root, and fast intrinsics. In
fp64 it reproduced the contraction column exactly, its single-precision
intrinsics not applying; in fp32 it gave $1.431\times10^{-6}$ and
$1.574\times10^{-5}$, both \emph{below} the contraction-only response, so the
more relaxed build sat closer to the host baseline and these transformations
partly cancel rather than accumulate. Which of them cancels was not isolated,
but flush-to-zero is the candidate these cases admit, since $\psi$, $v_z$ and
$B_z$ start identically zero.

Median wall-time ratios show a crossover, the GPU being slower for $800\times1$
Brio--Wu but about $6\times$ faster for $256^2$ Orszag--Tang, on a single-core
CPU baseline, so these are serial-to-device bounds rather than processor
comparisons. Brio--Wu leaves little useful device
work, so launches, transfers and output dominate where the larger mesh amortises
them: in end-to-end cell updates per second the CPU delivered 2.1--4.1 million
across both workloads while the GPU spanned 0.22 to 15.1 million. GPU fp64/fp32
median ratios of $1.146\times$ and $1.260\times$ sit far below the fp64 penalty
a consumer device imposes.

\section{Scale, time, and stochastic sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-scale-time-stochastic}

\subsection{Resolution dependence of discrepancies}
\label{sec:ch5-resolution-precision}

All eight self-refinement groups completed, and in every one the density
mean-$L_1$ precision discrepancy $D_N$, and therefore
$S_N=D_N/E^{64}_{256,512}$, rose monotonically from $128^2$ to $512^2$
(Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-precision-refinement}).
Refinement at fixed CFL takes more steps and resolves thinner Orszag--Tang (OT)
current sheets and sharper Kelvin--Helmholtz (KH) shear gradients. Even so the
largest value, $S_{512}=2.76\times10^{-2}$ for KH--HLLD, stayed below 3\% of the
matched fp64 finest adjacent-grid difference.
In units of $u_{32}$ the domain mean rises
from 2.31 to 54.84 for OT/HLL and from 12.96 to 335.46 for OT/HLLD, so the
few-roundings reading of Section~\ref{sec:ch5-cross-system} is a coarse-grid
statement: refinement amplifies round-off in the domain mean, not only locally,
the same trade the Alfv\'en ladder shows against an exact solution
(Section~\ref{sec:ch4-cp-alfven}). Three grids cannot fix the rate, so no
crossover grid is extrapolated.

\subsection{Growth of discrepancies with time}
\label{sec:ch5-temporal}

The temporal test fitted both models of Equation~\ref{eq:ch3-temporal-fit} to
the CPU HLL density discrepancies of Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-temporal} over the
fixed windows $[0.01,0.1]$ and $[0.1,0.5]$. On Brio--Wu the exponential gave
$\lambda=30.6153$ with root-mean-square log residual 0.143, while on the same 13
samples the power law gave $k=1.44$ with residual 0.064 and $R^2=0.993$ against
0.963. The $L_\infty$ series barely separates the models, 0.172 against 0.186.

In units of $u_{32}$ the Brio--Wu mean discrepancy rises from 0.098 to 2.351
across its window, whereas Orszag--Tang already stands at 1.756 at its first
sample and stays between about 2.1 and 2.6 thereafter, so any earlier rise went
unobserved. Both arrive at about 2.3 roundings, so the apparent reversal, a
one-dimensional shock tube separating faster than a chaotic two-dimensional
case, records where each series was sampled relative to that level rather than
anything about dimensionality. Orszag--Tang's near-zero slopes sit against RMS
log residuals of 0.039 and 0.201, so its mean series is a tight plateau while
its maximum is scattered.

Precision is not the only axis that grows. The same treatment was applied to
two build axes on the identical Brio--Wu fp32 configuration, each stopping time
again an independently executed pair (Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-impl-temporal}). At
nvcc's default the second of these is the CPU--GPU difference itself, so that
series records how far the two devices separate as the calculation runs.
Relaxed host math grew from $4.610\times10^{-9}$ to $1.176\times10^{-7}$ in
density mean $L_1$ over $t\in[0.01,0.1]$ and device contraction from
$2.990\times10^{-9}$ to $1.058\times10^{-7}$, both rising from about a tenth of
a rounding to $3.51\,u_{32}$ for the host axis and $3.16\,u_{32}$ for the
device axis, the domain-mean values carried into
Table~\ref{tab:ch6-axis-ranking}. Power laws again fit
better, at $k=1.39$ with $R^2=0.986$ for the host axis and $k=1.57$ with
$0.993$ for the device axis, against $0.950$ and $0.921$ for the exponentials.
All three axes therefore accumulate at a similar rate, $k\simeq1.4$--$1.6$.

The exponential model is imported from the perturbation-growth picture for
chaotic systems \citep{lorenz1963}, where a difference grows like
$\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}$ only while it stays small
\citep{boffettaEtAl2002predictability}; on every axis measured here it is the
weaker description. Extending the axis settles what the power laws measure.
Carried to $t=3.0$, past the roll-up its $t=1.0$ validation stopping time does
not reach, the Kelvin--Helmholtz discrepancy rises from $0.585$ to $2.902$
roundings and then flattens, and an early Orszag--Tang window gives $k=0.284$,
so the rise recorded above as unobserved is that approach sampled earlier.
Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-temporal-saturation} places all three cases on one scale:
each climbs to two or three roundings and stops. The exponent therefore reads
where a window sits on that approach rather than a growth law. None of these
series shows amplification, but all three share one grid and one flux.

\subsection{Grid and solver dependence of the saturation}
\label{sec:ch5-saturation-axes}

That saturation was measured on one configuration, $128^2$ HLL, while
Section~\ref{sec:ch5-resolution-precision} shows the same level rising steeply
with refinement. The identical Kelvin--Helmholtz series was therefore repeated
one changed factor at a time, on the same binaries, stopping times and analyser
(Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-saturation-axes}).

Lowering CFL to 0.2 raises the level from 2.90 to 4.45 roundings but leaves the
shape, the endpoint ratio $e(3.0)/e(0.2)$ reading 1.67 against the baseline 1.62
and the exponent $0.129\pm0.025$ against $0.143\pm0.011$, so the time step is
not the confound. The other two factors are, and they compound: refining to
$256^2$ takes the HLL exponent to $0.289\pm0.048$, replacing the clamped flux
with HLLD at a matched step takes it to $0.621\pm0.089$, and both together to
$0.751\pm0.165$, against endpoint ratios of 2.30, 6.39 and 8.78. Both HLLD
series leave the plateau after the roll-up, the finer reaching 82.7 roundings
where the baseline reaches 2.90.

That changes the conclusion, not its scale. Fitted over $t\geq1.5$ on the
twenty-sample $128^2$ HLLD series, the exponential of
Equation~\ref{eq:ch3-temporal-fit} beats the power law on the residual
criterion of Section~\ref{sec:ch3-metrics}, 0.059 against 0.064 at $R^2=0.983$,
giving $\lambda=0.948$ and an e-folding time of 1.06 code units. The margin is
narrow on twenty samples, so what the fit supports is that the least dissipative
path tested is the one configuration where accumulation no longer describes the
series better than amplification, rather than that amplification is established.
The eight-sample $256^2$ series holds four points in that window and does not
separate the models. Two limits remain. The window lies past the $t=1.0$
validation stopping time, and HLLD carries seven to seventeen times HLL's
cell-width-scaled divergence maximum there, so on the solver axis amplification
and weak divergence control are correlated. The grid axis separates them:
refining HLLD halves that maximum, $5.0\times10^{-2}$ to $2.5\times10^{-2}$ of
$B_0$, while its exponent rises, and refining HLL raises it by a quarter while
its exponent doubles, so the divergence residual does not track the growth.

\subsection{Monte Carlo arithmetic}
\label{sec:ch5-mca}

Verificarlo instrumentation \citep{denis_etal_2016} supplied the MCA
runs, randomly rounding each operation at a chosen virtual precision after
\citet{parker_1997}. The solver was rebuilt whole-program with
\texttt{verificarlo-c++}, so operations in its own translation units are
perturbed and library calls are not.
Table~\ref{tab:ch5-mca-primary} retains the matched Brio--Wu density
results at $N=800$, $t=0.1$, with 30 samples for each of HLL and HLLD. At
virtual p24, the maximum cellwise sample standard deviations were
$2.275\times10^{-6}$ and $4.007\times10^{-6}$; the corresponding deterministic
fp32--fp64 $L_\infty$ differences were $1.075\times10^{-6}$ and
$1.629\times10^{-6}$. Their quotients are 2.12 and 2.46.

The two statistics are not equivalent, but their order-one quotients place the
deterministic fp32--fp64 separation on the scale of the cloud from random
24-bit rounding, so a single fp32 endpoint undersamples rounding sensitivity.
At virtual p53 the HLL and HLLD spreads fell to $5.117\times10^{-15}$ and
$8.442\times10^{-15}$, and the matched fp64 \texttt{/fp:fast} responses of
Section~\ref{sec:ch5-build-sensitivity}, $1.887\times10^{-15}$ and
$4.413\times10^{-15}$, sit inside them, so the reading below is not an artefact
of the fp32 endpoint. No confidence interval is attached to these $n=30$ spreads
\citep{sohier_etal_2021}.

That scale bounds what the axis comparisons on this configuration can resolve.
Brio--Wu fp32 at $N=800$ carries four of them --- precision
$1.075\times10^{-6}$, relaxed host math $1.311\times10^{-6}$, device
contraction $2.265\times10^{-6}$ and a branch rule at zero --- and the HLL p24
spread exceeds every one. What survives the stochastic scale is the separation
of zero from non-zero, not the sequence among the non-zero responses.

\section{Robustness checks}
\label{sec:ch5-robustness}

\subsection{Thread count and CFL sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-thread-cfl}

The MHD sweeps carry no work-sharing directives
(Section~\ref{sec:ch2-execution}), so a thread request cannot reach their
arithmetic, and counts of 1, 2, 4 and 8 duly matched the one-thread
Orszag--Tang and KH states at zero ULP. That null describes the code rather
than threading, so the axis was repeated on the Euler path, whose sweeps and
update are wrapped in \texttt{omp parallel for} and whose CFL scan is an
explicit \texttt{reduction(max:\,\ldots)}. On Liska--Wendroff
Configuration~3 at $200^2$ the serial and 1-, 2-, 4- and 8-thread builds
returned bitwise identical saved states in both precisions while the fp64
median wall time fell from 1.31 to 0.27 seconds, a $4.79\times$ speed-up. The
threads did the work and still changed nothing:
sweep rows are independent and a maximum is exactly associative, so the
reordering that makes parallel summation non-deterministic
\citep{collange_etal_2015,demmel_nguyen_2013,diethelm2012limits} never arises.

All KH runs at CFL 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 completed with finite, positive
states, and Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-kh-cfl} shows the fp32--fp64 density
$L_\infty$ separation falling as CFL rises and the step count drops fourfold, by
5.25 for HLL and 2.28 for HLLD. Neither number is a mechanism: CFL 0.6 sits out
of order in both solvers at one run per setting, and changing CFL changes the
fp64 solution too, so each point differences a different matched pair. Since
refinement rates and the long-horizon shape are both insensitive to the setting,
it moves the level of the precision response without moving its behaviour.
