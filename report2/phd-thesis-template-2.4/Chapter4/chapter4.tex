\chapter{MHD Validation Results}
\label{chap:mhd-validation-results}

\section{Validation hierarchy overview}
\label{sec:ch4-validation-hierarchy}

Chapter~3 defines the reference hierarchy, completion checks, physical-state
checks, and divergence diagnostics used here. Validation proceeds from a
one-dimensional reference and two-dimensional property checks to matched
CPU/GPU, Orszag--Tang, and Kelvin--Helmholtz tests. Each result is conditional
on its solver, precision, device, grid, and final time, and is not transferable.

\section{One-dimensional Brio--Wu validation}
\label{sec:ch4-brio-wu}

The one-dimensional test uses the standard coplanar MHD Riemann problem of
\citet{brioWu1988}. Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-validation-refinement-glm}(a) compares
three fp64 grids with an aligned, block-averaged $N=8000$ fp64 numerical
reference. From $N=200$ to $N=800$, the density mean $L_1$ difference fell
from $1.481\times10^{-2}$ to $5.642\times10^{-3}$, while $L_2$ fell from
$3.642\times10^{-2}$ to $1.923\times10^{-2}$. Both decreased monotonically;
the observed three-grid rates were 0.70 and 0.46, respectively. All candidate
runs retained finite states, with $\mathrm{divB}_{\max}\leq4.441\times10^{-14}$.
The refinement moved the computed state towards the defined numerical reference,
but this is not an error comparison with an exact Brio--Wu solution. The rates
describe only these three grids; they do not establish an asymptotic regime for
the discontinuous solution.

\section{Two-dimensional invariance and divergence control}
\label{sec:ch4-invariance-glm}

An $800\times4$ embedding of the Brio--Wu problem tested whether the
two-dimensional update preserved a state that is constant in the transverse
direction. The maximum transverse deviation was zero. Against the corresponding
one-dimensional run, the mean and maximum absolute density differences were
$3.550\times10^{-4}$ and $7.034\times10^{-3}$; the corresponding gates were
$10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}$.
The separate periodic-disturbance test in
Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-validation-refinement-glm}(b) examines the GLM family of
\citet{dedner2002glm}. At $t=0.5$, $\max|\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{B}|$ was 3.030
without damping, compared with 0.2678 for $c_r=0.18$ and 0.8429 for
$c_r=0.36$. Both non-zero settings reduced the terminal maximum in this sweep;
the smaller value at $c_r=0.18$ does not establish a globally optimal parameter.
The two tests isolate different properties: transverse preservation by the
directional update and the cleaning subsystem's response to non-zero divergence.

\section{Matched CPU/GPU implementation validation}
\label{sec:ch4-cpu-gpu-validation}

The matched comparison asks whether changing device altered the saved state
when the case, HLL solver, precision, and configuration were fixed.
Table~\ref{tab:ch4-cpu-gpu} reports identical CPU/GPU step counts for
Brio--Wu at $800\times1$ and Orszag--Tang at $256^2$. In fp32 and fp64, all
four saved-state comparisons gave zero maximum ULP and zero absolute
$L_\infty$ difference; the repeated hardware runs retained the same result.
This validates the bounded local CUDA path for these four combinations. The
implementation has the same broad second-order MUSCL--Hancock/CUDA setting as
the GPU MHD study of \citet{bardDorelli2014gpu}, but the result here is a
correctness comparison rather than a portable speed-up claim. It does not cover
HLLD, Kelvin--Helmholtz, GPU MCA, or other devices. Bit identity applies to the
stored conservative variables; unsaved intermediate stages were not compared.

\section{Orszag--Tang validation}
\label{sec:ch4-orszag-tang}

The Orszag--Tang test uses the periodic unit-square rescaling of
\citet{toth2000divb}. The HLL runs at $128^2$, $256^2$, and $512^2$ completed
with finite, positive physical states. Their mean divergence diagnostic
decreased from 0.1364 to 0.1036 as the grid was refined, while the local
maximum increased from 1.734 to 7.729 as thinner current sheets were resolved.
The audited fp64 HLL $256^2$--$512^2$ pair also retained zero relative mass
drift. Its temperature field preserved the interacting-shock and current-sheet
topology shown in Orszag--Tang
calculations \citep{toth2000divb,bardDorelli2014gpu}; this is qualitative
support rather than a field-wise reference comparison. Thus refinement lowered
the domain mean while sharpening local peaks; the maximum alone does not
describe global control.

Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-resolution-precision} gives the adjacent-grid density mean
$L_1$ diagnostic. For fp64 HLL, the $128^2$--$256^2$ and
$256^2$--$512^2$ differences were 0.1203 and 0.07722, giving
$p\simeq0.639$. The corresponding HLLD \citep{miyoshiKusano2005hlld}
differences were 0.07515 and 0.04182, giving $p\simeq0.846$; the fp32 rates
agreed to the displayed precision.
The HLLD fp64 run at $512^2$ completed at $t=0.5$ in 3277 steps; its saved
state remained finite with $\rho_{\min}=1.098$ and $p_{\min}=0.380$. Across the
matrix, all 24 planned runs and all eight three-grid groups completed, and each
group produced a positive density mean-$L_1$ self-refinement diagnostic.
The different HLL and HLLD CFL values preclude a solver ranking from these
numbers. This evidence supports bounded completion and self-consistency rather
than agreement with an exact two-dimensional state.

\section{Kelvin--Helmholtz validation}
\label{sec:ch4-kelvin-helmholtz}

The project-defined Kelvin--Helmholtz case uses a smooth, periodic MHD
double-shear form adapted from \citet{tricco2019kh} and an aligned weak-field
setting motivated by \citet{frankEtAl1996kh}. The reference study of
\citet{lecoanetEtAl2016kh} supplies an important interpretation boundary:
morphological structure alone is not an accuracy measure for nonlinear KH
evolution. All HLL and HLLD, fp32 and fp64 runs completed at $128^2$, $256^2$,
and $512^2$ with finite, positive states. For fp64, the HLL mean divergence
diagnostic decreased from $6.877\times10^{-5}$ to $2.305\times10^{-5}$, while
its local maximum changed from $6.029\times10^{-4}$ to $9.236\times10^{-4}$.
The HLLD mean decreased from $2.747\times10^{-4}$ to $3.479\times10^{-5}$,
and its local maximum from $9.023\times10^{-3}$ to $5.149\times10^{-3}$.
The audited fp64 HLL $256^2$--$512^2$ pair retained zero relative mass drift.
Mean and maximum diagnostics are interpreted separately and do not prove a
divergence-free solution.

For fp64 HLL, the two adjacent-grid density mean $L_1$ differences were
$3.473\times10^{-3}$ and $1.836\times10^{-3}$, giving $p=0.919$. For fp64
HLLD, they were $2.309\times10^{-4}$ and $8.496\times10^{-5}$, giving
$p=1.442$; the fp32 rates were 0.919 and 1.436. Each rate belongs to a fixed
solver, precision, and CFL group. Because HLL and HLLD used different CFL
values, these successful within-group trends do not establish a solver ranking.

\section{Validation limits}
\label{sec:ch4-validation-limits}

The Brio--Wu comparison uses a high-resolution numerical reference, whereas
the two-dimensional cases rely on self-reference, physical-state checks,
and divergence diagnostics. Three grids show the
direction of an engineering trend but do not establish an asymptotic regime
for solutions containing shocks or fine current sheets. A resolution-sensitive
local divergence maximum cannot by itself establish vanishing global divergence
error. The GPU evidence covers
only HLL Brio--Wu and Orszag--Tang. The two-dimensional comparisons use the mean
norms defined in Chapter~3, and their raw central-difference divergence values
are grid-scale diagnostics rather than dimensionless errors. These boundaries
set the interpretation range for the systematic sensitivity results in
Chapter~5.
