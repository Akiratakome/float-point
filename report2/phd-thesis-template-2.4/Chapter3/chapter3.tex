\chapter{Experimental Design and Reproducibility Methodology}

\section{Research questions and controlled axes}

This chapter operationalises the four questions in
Chapter~\ref{chap:introduction}. Research
Question~\ref{rq:mhd-validation} uses the reference hierarchy and predeclared
pass/fail validation criteria for the CPU and tested HLL CUDA paths. Research
Question~\ref{rq:controlled-variation} maps implementation axes to matched
saved states and repeated solver/device timing groups. Research
Question~\ref{rq:scale-time-mca} adds resolution, observation time and
separately scoped virtual-precision Monte Carlo arithmetic (MCA) comparisons. Research
Question~\ref{rq:reproducibility-record} sets the metadata and retention rules.
Primary axes are precision, build semantics, solver, device, resolution and
time; thread count and CFL test robustness. Every reported comparison is bound
to its case, grid, solver, precision, final time and baseline, and no
observation transfers between unmatched configurations.
The single-node experiments isolate these axes; MPI communication, partitioning
and reduction order form a separate future parallelism axis.

\section{Build and run matrix}

State arrays, fluxes, wave-speed estimates and the CFL calculation follow the
build precision, while the time accumulator is binary64 in both builds, so that
$t \leftarrow t+\Delta t$ does not lose small increments in fp32; the fp32
build is a declared mixed-precision configuration, not a uniform binary32
program. Matched builds vary the harness-recorded optimisation,
floating-point-math and branch-rule semantics; directory names do not prove
compiler behaviour. In MSVC terms O2 selects /O2, O3 selects /Ox and the
composite Ofast selects /Ox /fp:fast, which is why it confounds optimisation
with math semantics. The
isolated MSVC matrix compares /O2 with /Ox, default math with /fp:fast at /O2,
and $\leq$ with $<$ at /O2 and default math. Virtual p53 and p24 MCA perturb
operations at 53 or 24 significand bits.

\section{Reference hierarchy and validation criteria}
\label{sec:ch3-reference-hierarchy}

Unit tests, transverse invariance and CPU/GPU agreement verify implementation
properties, not ideal MHD against physical reality
\citep{oberkampfRoy2010vv}. Evidence is ordered by independence: external
fieldwise comparison where available, high-resolution comparison,
self-refinement, then property checks. Brio--Wu uses an aligned fp64 $N=8000$
comparator, not an exact solution; block averaging aligns adjacent
two-dimensional grids. The grids are not fine enough for a leading truncation
term to dominate, so fitted orders are descriptive rather than Richardson
extrapolation or Grid Convergence Index (GCI) estimates
\citep{roache1994gci}. Completion requires
the declared final time and output; positive states require finite variables
with $\rho,p>0$; divergence checks track departures from
$\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf B=0$.

Brio--Wu \citep{brioWu1988} has outflow boundaries; the two-dimensional cases
are doubly periodic with $\gamma=5/3$:
\begin{equation}
 (\rho,v_x,p,B_y)=
 \begin{cases}
  (1,\;0,\;1,\;1), & x<x_0,\\
  (0.125,\;0,\;0.1,\;-1), & x\ge x_0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \begin{aligned}
  &x\in[0,1],\; x_0=0.5,\; \gamma=2,\; t=0.1,\\
  &B_x=0.75,\; v_y=v_z=B_z=\psi=0 .
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ch3-ic-briowu}
\end{equation}
For Orszag--Tang at $t=0.5$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \rho &= \gamma^{2}=25/9,\qquad p=\gamma=5/3,\\
  \mathbf v &= (-\sin 2\pi y,\;\sin 2\pi x),\\
  \mathbf B &= B_0(-\sin 2\pi y,\;\sin 4\pi x),\qquad B_0=1,\\
  &v_z=B_z=\psi=0,\qquad c_r=0.18,\qquad
  (x,y)\in[0,1]^2\ \text{(periodic)} .
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ch3-ic-ot}
\end{equation}
Under $X=2\pi x$, $Y=2\pi y$, $T=2\pi t$, this is the original
$[0,2\pi]^2$ benchmark at $t=0.5$ ($T=\pi$) \citep{orszagTang1979} in the
normalisation of \citet{toth2000divb}. Its separated field dependencies,
$B_x(y)$ and $B_y(x)$, give exactly zero cell-centred divergence initially;
later values measure numerical divergence. The project-defined smooth
Kelvin--Helmholtz double shear layer at $t=1.0$ is
\begin{align}
 &(x,y)\in[0,1]^2\ \text{(periodic)},\quad
 \rho=p=1,\quad \gamma=5/3,\quad c_r=0.18, \notag\\
 v_x &= U_0\left[\tanh\frac{y-y_1}{a}-\tanh\frac{y-y_2}{a}-1\right], \notag\\
 v_y &= \delta\sin(2\pi x)\left[\mathrm{e}^{-(y-y_1)^2/s^2}\right. \notag\\
     &\hspace{5.6em}\left.{}+\mathrm{e}^{-(y-y_2)^2/s^2}\right], \notag\\
 &(y_1,y_2)=(0.25,0.75),\quad U_0=0.5,\quad a=0.025, \notag\\
 &\delta=0.01,\quad s=0.05,\quad B_x=B_0=0.1, \notag\\
 &v_z=B_y=B_z=\psi=0,
 \label{eq:ch3-ic-kh}
\end{align}
This gives a uniform flow-aligned field at Alfv\'en Mach number $5$ and an
exactly divergence-free initial state.

The refinement ladder uses $\mathrm{CFL}=0.4$ for HLL and $0.2$ for HLLD; the
reason for the HLLD setting appears in Section~\ref{sec:ch4-orszag-tang}. This
asymmetry prevents direct cross-solver comparison of refinement diagnostics.

\section{Metrics and statistical treatment}
\label{sec:ch3-metrics}

For a same-grid difference $d_{ij}=q_{ij}-q^{\mathrm{ref}}_{ij}$ over
$N=N_xN_y$ cells, the reported mean norms are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 L_{1,\mathrm{mean}}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{ij}|d_{ij}|,\\
 L_{2,\mathrm{mean}}&=\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{ij}d_{ij}^{2}\right)^{1/2},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ch3-mean-norms}
\end{equation}
with $L_\infty=\max_{ij}|d_{ij}|$. Physical-domain integrals instead use
$\Delta x\Delta y$. The compact cross-system matrix uses the mean-relative
density difference
\begin{equation}
 L_{1,\mathrm{rel}}(\rho)=
 \frac{N^{-1}\sum_{ij}|\rho_{ij}-\rho^{\mathrm{ref}}_{ij}|}
      {N^{-1}\sum_{ij}|\rho^{\mathrm{ref}}_{ij}|},
 \label{eq:ch3-mean-relative}
\end{equation}
when its denominator is finite and positive, and only within that matrix.
For binary32 the unit roundoff is
$u_{32}=2^{-24}\approx5.96\times10^{-8}$ \citep{ieee7542019}; discrepancies are
also reported as $L_{1,\mathrm{rel}}/u_{32}$, so a value near unity means the
two calculations differ by about one rounding of the field, and larger values
indicate accumulation or amplification. Here $L_{1,\mathrm{rel}}$ is
normalised by the reference field mean whereas $L_\infty$ is absolute, so the
two are not on a common scale and their ratio does not by itself measure
localisation.
Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-resolution-precision} uses block-averaged absolute density
$L_{1,\mathrm{mean}}$. Its descriptive diagnostic is
\begin{equation}
 p_{\mathrm{obs}}=\log_2\!\left(\frac{E_{128,256}}{E_{256,512}}\right).
 \label{eq:ch3-observed-p}
\end{equation}
For Chapter~5, let $D_N=\|\rho_{32,N}-\rho_{64,N}\|_{1,\mathrm{mean}}$ and let
$E^{64}_{256,512}$ be the matched, block-averaged fp64 adjacent-grid
mean-$L_1$ disagreement. The diagnostic
$S_N=D_N/E^{64}_{256,512}$ does not measure exact error, GCI uncertainty or
fp32 adequacy.

The divergence diagnostic applies centred differences on interior cells,
\begin{align}
 D_{ij} &=\frac{B_{x,i+1,j}-B_{x,i-1,j}}{2\Delta x}
          +\frac{B_{y,i,j+1}-B_{y,i,j-1}}{2\Delta y}, \notag\\
 \mathrm{divB}_{\mathrm{mean}}
        &=\frac{1}{N_I}\sum_{(i,j)\in I}|D_{ij}|,
 &\mathrm{divB}_{\max}
        &=\max_{(i,j)\in I}|D_{ij}|.
 \label{eq:ch3-divb}
\end{align}
where $I$ excludes the outer stencil layer. These have dimensions of magnetic
field per length and measure the absolute residual of the discrete solenoidal
constraint. Small values indicate control of the discrete constraint; they do
not establish magnetic-field accuracy.
Same-precision CPU/GPU comparisons use absolute $L_\infty$ and maximum distance
in units in the last place (ULP) on saved conservative states. Sign-adjusted
bit patterns of the same
IEEE type map to monotone integers, giving the largest cellwise distance. Zero
ULP denotes bitwise equality; ULP is neither a cross-precision
nor a physical error norm.

For $n$ MCA samples, the per-cell sample standard deviation is
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_j=\left[\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{s=1}^{n}
 (q_{s,j}-\bar q_j)^2\right]^{1/2},\qquad
 \mathrm{spread}_q=\max_j\sigma_j .
 \label{eq:ch3-mca-spread}
\end{equation}
The maximum conservatively aggregates shocks and shear layers. The numerical
signal-to-noise ratio is
$\operatorname{mean}_j|\bar q_j|/\operatorname{mean}_j\sigma_j$, not the physical
turbulence signal-to-noise ratio.

Elapsed time is subprocess wall time from solver launch through completion and
required binary output, not kernel time. The KH solver/precision groups discard
one CPU warm-up and retain $n=5$ measurements; the CPU/GPU packet retains
$n=5$ per group but excludes no device warm-up or outlier. Both report the
median and interquartile range ($\mathrm{IQR}=Q_{75}-Q_{25}$). Matched-median
ratios apply only to
the recorded workstation, not performance portability.

The temporal experiment aligns fp32 and fp64 saved states at each time and
computes density $L_{1,\mathrm{mean}}$ and $L_\infty$. For positive samples in
the predeclared case-specific window, ordinary least squares fits
\begin{equation}
 \log e(t)=a+\lambda t .
 \label{eq:ch3-temporal-fit}
\end{equation}
Here $t$ is code time, so $\lambda$ has inverse-code-time units; for an adequate
positive fit, $1/\lambda$ is the e-folding time. $R^2$, root-mean-square log
residual and maximum absolute log residual assess the fixed-window model; the
last two report typical and worst log-space departures.
Ordinary least squares assumes
uncorrelated residuals with roughly constant variance. A near-zero $R^2$ does
not separate a flat series from a noisy one, since it only records the absence
of a slope; the root-mean-square log residual separates a plateau from
scatter. Thus $\lambda$ is a
descriptive rate, not a physical instability
rate; no confidence interval is claimed.

\section{Harness, metadata, and retention}
\label{sec:ch3-harness-metadata}

Experiments follow configuration, build, run, measurement, aggregation and
plotting. Each record identifies configuration and commit; applicable
configuration and binary hashes; compiler and effective floating-point
semantics; recorded environment; and requested final time, return status,
completion diagnostics and wall time. These fields distinguish complete from
truncated runs.

Aggregation checks matrix completeness and physical admissibility before
summaries or figures. Logs, configurations, metadata, scalar summaries and
figures are retained; large grids only when needed to revalidate a comparison.
This evidence permits audit without preserving transient fields.
