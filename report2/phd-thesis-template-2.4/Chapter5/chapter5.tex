\section{Deterministic output sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-deterministic-sensitivity}

\subsection{Euler--MHD cross-system sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-cross-system}

All sixteen Sod, LW3, Brio--Wu, and Orszag--Tang runs in
Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-cross-system} completed. The O2-default fp32--fp64 series
spans $1.377\times10^{-7}$--$3.762\times10^{-7}$ in density mean-relative
$L_1$ and $3.837\times10^{-7}$--$5.254\times10^{-6}$ in $L_\infty$, which is
absolute rather than mean-relative.

In units of the binary32 unit roundoff $u_{32}$ the O2-default domain-mean
differences are 2.46 for Sod, 6.31 for LW3, 2.35 for Brio--Wu and 2.31 for
Orszag--Tang. These four cases therefore differ by between two and seven
binary32 roundings after a complete simulation: in the domain mean the
updates deliver about the unit
roundoff of the working precision and do not amplify it at these resolutions,
although the comparison does not isolate the mechanism
\citep{higham2002accuracy}. Amplification appears only in $L_\infty$, whose
$5.254\times10^{-6}$ maximum likely reflects local structures collecting
differences.

\subsection{Deterministic fp32--fp64 sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-deterministic-precision}

Beyond the Brio--Wu and Orszag--Tang $L_\infty$ values of
$1.075\times10^{-6}$ and $3.565\times10^{-6}$ in
Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-cross-system}, a standalone same-grid comparison at
$256^2$, $t=1.0$ ran Kelvin--Helmholtz with both solvers at CFL 0.4, giving
$1.786\times10^{-6}$ and $3.230\times10^{-6}$; the resolution ladder of
Section~\ref{sec:ch4-orszag-tang} is a separate experiment in which HLLD used
CFL 0.2. All runs completed with finite, positive states, so these
$10^{-6}$-scale local responses show sensitivity without precision-triggered
solver failure.

\subsection{Effective build-semantics sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-build-sensitivity}

The build pair in Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-cross-system} compared the O2-default
profile with Ofast-fast, which raises the optimisation level and relaxes
floating-point semantics together, so the comparison confounds the two. In
fp64, the mean-relative density response lay between $4.24\times10^{-17}$ and
$4.21\times10^{-16}$; in fp32 it ranged from exactly zero for Sod to
$5.124\times10^{-7}$ for LW3. For LW3 in fp32 that value exceeds the same
case's $3.762\times10^{-7}$ fp32--fp64 difference, so for that case relaxed
build semantics moved the stored density further than halving the working
precision did.

At $N=800$, $t=0.1$, CFL 0.4 and one thread, a Microsoft Visual C++
(MSVC)~19.51 Brio--Wu rebuild varied each axis separately and compared
saved density. All four /Ox--/O2 discrepancies were zero, so optimisation
level alone left the output unchanged.
By contrast, /fp:fast versus compiler-default math
\citep{microsoftMsvcFp2021} gave $L_\infty$ values of
$1.887\times10^{-15}$ and $1.311\times10^{-6}$ for HLL, and
$4.413\times10^{-15}$ and $1.729\times10^{-6}$ for HLLD, in fp64 and fp32.
The nonzero values show that relaxed transformations changed the numerical
path, and the larger fp32 response is consistent with its coarser unit
roundoff. Changing
$\leq$ to $<$ gave zero for both HLL groups, whereas HLLD produced
$3.886\times10^{-15}$ and $2.608\times10^{-6}$. The HLL zero is structural: the global GLM speed clamps its signal speeds, so
$S_L^{H}<0<S_R^{H}$ always holds and neither outer dispatch branch can be
selected (Section~\ref{sec:ch2-riemann}). HLLD keeps the unclamped Davis speeds
and three further ties, at $S_L^*$, $S_R^*$ and $S_M$, across each of which the
flux is value-continuous, so the variants diverge only where rounding decides
the side of an exact zero.

A further pair held optimisation, floating-point model, branch rule, solver,
grid and CFL fixed and moved only the MSVC baseline instruction set from
/arch:SSE2 to /arch:AVX2. All four solver--precision groups stayed bitwise
identical even though the two binaries differed by hash. Widening the vector
instruction set therefore changed the generated code without changing the
arithmetic, as /fp:precise forbids the reassociation that wider registers would
otherwise invite.

\section{Solver and device performance}
\label{sec:ch5-performance}

\subsection{CPU timing: HLL versus HLLD}
\label{sec:ch5-solver-timing}

Changing HLL to HLLD changes the
numerical method, so Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-kh-timing} compares CPU cost within a fixed
$256^2$ Kelvin--Helmholtz configuration at $t=1.0$, CFL 0.4, and
\texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS}=1. The fp64 medians were 34.484~s
for HLL and 39.542~s for HLLD; fp32 medians were 29.196~s and 34.254~s. All
groups took 1148 steps. FP32 was $1.181\times$ faster for HLL and
$1.154\times$ for HLLD; HLLD/HLL median cost ratios were 1.147 in fp64 and
1.173 in fp32.

A fixed-width vector register holds twice as many fp32 as fp64 values, yet
speed-ups of 1.181 and 1.154 remain well below $2\times$, so the limits are
bandwidth and scalar or non-vectorised work rather than arithmetic
throughput \citep{williamsEtAl2009roofline}. HLLD's modest 15--17\% premium,
despite three extra waves and fourteen degeneracy guards per interface,
supports the same reading.

\subsection{Matched CPU/GPU agreement and performance}
\label{sec:ch5-hardware}

The same-precision CPU/GPU saved states in Table~\ref{tab:ch4-cpu-gpu} were
bit-identical in both maximum ULP and absolute $L_\infty$, and the $n=5$
groups in Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-hardware} reproduce that result.

That agreement is arranged by one device flag, so the flag was varied.
Rebuilding only the CUDA kernel translation unit with nvcc's default
\texttt{--fmad=true}, and holding host binary, case, grid, precision and update
order fixed, removed bitwise agreement in all four pairs. The density
$L_\infty$ differences were $1.665\times10^{-15}$ and $2.309\times10^{-14}$ in
fp64 for Brio--Wu and Orszag--Tang, and $2.265\times10^{-6}$ and
$2.074\times10^{-5}$ in fp32. The Brio--Wu fp32 value shares its case, grid,
solver and final time with two axes already reported, and exceeds both: the
$1.075\times10^{-6}$ fp32--fp64 difference and the $1.311\times10^{-6}$
/fp:fast response. On that workload one device compiler default moved the
stored field further than halving the working precision did.

Median CPU/GPU wall-time
ratios show the crossover: the GPU was slower for $800\times1$
Brio--Wu, but about $6\times$ faster for $256^2$ Orszag--Tang.

Brio--Wu's small workload leaves little useful device work, so context
creation, per-step kernel launches, host--device transfers and output can
dominate, whereas the larger Orszag--Tang mesh amortises those fixed costs.
Expressed as end-to-end cell updates per second, which is portable across
machines in a way seconds are not, the CPU delivered 2.1--4.1 million across
both workloads while the GPU spanned 0.22 to 15.1 million, a seventyfold
range on the same kernel. This scale dependence is consistent with a related
GPU MUSCL--Hancock MHD solver
\citep{bardDorelli2014gpu,williamsEtAl2009roofline}. Within the GPU results,
fp64/fp32 median ratios gave speed-ups of $1.146\times$ for Brio--Wu and
$1.260\times$ for Orszag--Tang, far smaller than the fp64 penalty a consumer
device imposes, which again places the kernel away from a floating-point
bound.

\section{Scale, time, and stochastic sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-scale-time-stochastic}

\subsection{Resolution dependence of discrepancies}
\label{sec:ch5-resolution-precision}

All eight self-refinement groups were complete. For both Orszag--Tang (OT) and
Kelvin--Helmholtz (KH), each HLL/HLLD combination showed a density mean-$L_1$
precision discrepancy $D_N$ that increased monotonically from $128^2$ to
$512^2$. Because $E^{64}_{256,512}$ was fixed
within each group, $S_N=D_N/E^{64}_{256,512}$ rose likewise.

Refinement at fixed CFL requires more steps, and hence more rounding
operations, per unit of simulated time, and resolves thinner OT current sheets
and sharper KH shear gradients where perturbations can localise. Even the
largest value, $S_{512}=2.76\times10^{-2}$ for KH--HLLD,
remained below 3\% of the matched fp64 finest adjacent-grid difference. The
largest local response occurred for OT--HLLD at $512^2$:
$L_\infty=5.031\times10^{-2}$ while mean-$L_1=5.554\times10^{-5}$.

Expressed in units of $u_{32}$, the domain-mean discrepancy rises from 2.31 at
$128^2$ to 54.84 at $512^2$ for OT/HLL and from 12.96 to 335.46 for OT/HLLD,
so the few-roundings reading of Section~\ref{sec:ch5-cross-system} is a
coarse-grid statement: refinement amplifies round-off in the domain mean, not
only locally. Fitting $S_N\propto N^{a}$ across the three grids and solving
$S_N=1$ puts the crossover, at which the discrepancy would reach the matched
fp64 finest adjacent-grid scale, at of order $10^4$ cells per side for HLLD and
between $10^4$ and $10^5$ for HLL. That is a three-point power-law
extrapolation over more than a decade, and the OT/HLL exponent is dominated by
a single factor-11.5 jump between $256^2$ and $512^2$. Lacking a
fallback-activation counter, the HLLD entries describe HLLD together with its
first-order fallback policy rather than HLLD alone.

\subsection{Growth of discrepancies with time}
\label{sec:ch5-temporal}

The temporal test fitted $\log e(t)=a+\lambda t$ to the CPU HLL density
discrepancies of Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-temporal} over the fixed windows
$[0.01,0.1]$ and $[0.1,0.5]$. The Brio--Wu mean-$L_1$ fit gave
$\lambda=30.6153$, hence $\exp(\lambda\Delta t)\simeq16$ across the window,
against an endpoint ratio of about 24.

In units of $u_{32}$ the Brio--Wu mean discrepancy rises from 0.098 at
$t=0.01$ to 2.351 at $t=0.1$, whereas Orszag--Tang already stands at 1.756 at
its first sample, $t=0.05$, reaches 2.341 at $t=1.0$ and stays between about
2.1 and 2.6 throughout, so any earlier rise went unobserved. Both series
therefore arrive at about 2.3 roundings of the field at the resolutions used
here ($N=800$ and $128^2$), and the apparent reversal, a one-dimensional shock
tube separating faster than a chaotic two-dimensional case, is a statement
about where each series was
sampled relative to that level rather than about dimensionality.
Orszag--Tang's near-zero slopes, 0.02934
and $-0.04223$, sit against RMS log residuals of 0.039 for the mean and 0.201
for $L_\infty$, so the mean series is a tight plateau while the maximum is
scattered. That level does not survive refinement
(Section~\ref{sec:ch5-resolution-precision}).

The exponential model is imported from the perturbation-growth picture for
chaotic systems, where a difference grows like $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}$ only
while it stays small. A bounded norm cannot grow so indefinitely, and neither
series does: for Brio--Wu, $1/\lambda\simeq0.033$ compresses a crossing from a
tenth of a rounding to a few roundings. Brio--Wu is not chaotic either, so the
fitted rate measures accumulation of a spatially organised difference rather
than an instability.

\subsection{Monte Carlo arithmetic}
\label{sec:ch5-mca}

Verificarlo instrumentation \citep{denisEtAl2016verificarlo} supplied the MCA
runs, randomly rounding each operation at a chosen virtual precision after
\citet{parker1997mca}. For the related case of stochastic rounding, randomising
the rounding direction turns the worst-case $n\,u$ error bound of a
length-$n$ accumulation into a $\sqrt{n}\,u$ bound holding with high
probability \citep{crociEtAl2022stochastic}.
Table~\ref{tab:ch5-mca-primary} retains the matched Brio--Wu density
results at $N=800$, $t=0.1$, with 30 samples for each of HLL and HLLD. At
virtual p24, the maximum cellwise sample standard deviations were
$2.275\times10^{-6}$ and $4.007\times10^{-6}$; the corresponding deterministic
fp32--fp64 $L_\infty$ differences were $1.075\times10^{-6}$ and
$1.629\times10^{-6}$. Their quotients are 2.12 and 2.46.

These ratios divide a maximum cellwise sample standard deviation by a single
deterministic $L_\infty$ difference, but their order-one values place the
deterministic fp32--fp64 separation on the scale of the cloud from random
24-bit rounding, so a single fp32 endpoint undersamples rounding sensitivity.
At virtual p53, the spreads fell to $5.117\times10^{-15}$ and
$8.442\times10^{-15}$, eight to nine orders below p24.

\section{Robustness checks}
\label{sec:ch5-robustness}

\subsection{Requested-thread null control and CFL sensitivity}
\label{sec:ch5-thread-cfl}

The MHD sweeps contain no OpenMP work-sharing directives, so requested counts
of 1, 2, 4 and 8 changed neither work decomposition nor operation order, and
all matched the one-thread Orszag--Tang and KH states at zero ULP. The only
reduction in this explicit update is the stepwise maximum taken for the CFL
condition and for
$c_h$, and maximum over finite IEEE-754 values is exactly associative
(Section~\ref{sec:ch2-execution}), so even a genuinely parallel sweep could not
reorder it into a different answer. The reduction-order non-determinism
reported for parallel summation \citep{collangeEtAl2015reduction} would need a
summation reduction, which this scheme carries only in its diagnostics; so the
axis is uninformative here.

All KH runs at CFL 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, and 0.8 completed with finite, positive
states for both HLL and HLLD. Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-kh-cfl} shows the fp32--fp64
density $L_\infty$ separation falling as CFL rises in both solvers: for HLL by
5.25 between CFL 0.2 and 0.8, against a fourfold drop in fp64 step count. Lower
CFL reduces the time step and the truncation introduced per step, but requires
more steps and hence more rounding opportunities, so the separation tracks the
step count. CFL 0.6 sits out of order
in both solvers; with one run per setting, that non-monotonic point is
indistinguishable from run-to-run scatter.

\section{Summary across tested configurations}
\label{sec:ch5-bounded-summary}

Every discrepancy, separation and growth reported here is the difference
between two runs of the same solver on the same grid, differing only in the
declared axis: not a physical growth, not an instability, and not an error
against a reference solution.

In those terms, zero differences in within-group repeats, matched
CPU/GPU pairs and requested-thread controls establish bitwise reproduction
for those configurations. Precision, build semantics and CFL changes
produced non-zero, configuration-dependent separation; all 12 mean-$L_1$
precision discrepancies stayed below 3\% of their matched fp64 finest
refinement scale, yet grew steadily under refinement in units of $u_{32}$.
Both temporal series approached a common level of a few binary32 roundings,
differing only in where each was sampled relative to it, and hardware timing
depended on workload.
