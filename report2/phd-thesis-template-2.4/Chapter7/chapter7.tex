\section{Aim and evidence base}

This report tested effects of precision, build semantics, solver and execution
on ideal-MHD HRSC calculations and their reproducibility.
Precision and build changes produced detectable responses, yet the covered
CPU/GPU runs saved identical output and performance depended on workload.

\section{Contribution and claim boundaries}

The studied second-order finite-volume MUSCL--Hancock/GLM configurations with
HLL and HLLD completed with finite, positive states, which supports stability
over the tested horizons but not insensitivity to arithmetic detail. Neither
solver was uniformly the more robust, and the mechanisms behind that are set
out in Section~\ref{sec:ch6-relative-importance} rather than repeated here.

The principal methodological contribution is an experimental design organised
by test case and controlled axis.
It combines validation and matched variants with ULP and norm comparisons,
Monte Carlo arithmetic, temporal and performance diagnostics, metadata and
explicit claim boundaries. It keeps numerical-reference error, cross-variant
discrepancy, stochastic spread and runtime separate rather than treating them
as interchangeable measures of ``accuracy''.

\section{Prioritised future work}

The first priority is to establish reference problems that expose convergence
more directly than the present discontinuous and instability-dominated tests.
A smooth manufactured MHD solution or linear wave with a known convergence
rate should be paired with a matched, independent-code two-dimensional
benchmark. Comparisons should cover density, momentum, total energy, magnetic
field and derived pressure, together with conservation residuals and a
dimensionless divergence measure. A cheaper step in the same direction is a
quadruple-precision one-dimensional Brio--Wu reference, which would turn the
present same-scheme $N=8000$ comparator into an arithmetic ground truth and
separate discretisation error from precision discrepancy without new physics.

The next priority is to test the proposed mechanism for solver-dependent
sensitivity. HLLD should record wave-selection, guard, fallback and branch-hit
counts and their spatial locations; changes in those records can then be
correlated with peaks in the $L_\infty$ discrepancy. The build matrix should
also extend beyond the single MSVC environment, so that effects of compiler,
optimisation and floating-point semantics can be distinguished rather than
represented by one toolchain.

Execution coverage should then be made genuinely matched: GPU HLLD and
Kelvin--Helmholtz paths, complete matched Kelvin--Helmholtz temporal and MCA
experiments, actual OpenMP work sharing and distributed-memory Message Passing
Interface (MPI) reductions with controlled schedules and reduction orders, and
repetition on several recorded CPU and GPU systems. Performance measurements
should use device-appropriate warm-up, more timed samples, and separate kernel,
launch, transfer and I/O costs, with hardware counters and a Roofline analysis
to test whether bandwidth, instruction mix or launch overhead explains the
observed speedups.

Only then should the study broaden to methods with different dissipation and
constraint treatment: higher-order weighted essentially non-oscillatory (WENO)
or discontinuous Galerkin schemes, and constrained transport alongside GLM
cleaning. Resistive or relativistic MHD would test whether the conclusions
survive a change of physical system.
