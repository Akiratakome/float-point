\chapter{Discussion: Reproducibility Across Implementations}
\label{chap:reproducibility-discussion}

\section{Meaning of the validation evidence}
\label{sec:ch6-validation-meaning}

The validation hierarchy in Chapter~\ref{chap:mhd-validation-results} bounds
what the sensitivity results in Chapter~\ref{chap:variation-results} can mean.
None of its checks found the implementation failures they target, and none
measures physical accuracy, so a matched difference that clears completion and
physical-state screening is a response to the declared axis with its mechanism
left open. Validation makes the later comparisons interpretable without turning
every discrepancy into numerical error.

\section{Relative importance of the tested axes}
\label{sec:ch6-relative-importance}

Importance is case- and criterion-specific, and the two norms differ in what
they report. A small domain average can hide a large local one:
Orszag--Tang/HLLD at $512^2$ gave $L_\infty=5.031\times10^{-2}$ against a
mean-$L_1$ of $5.554\times10^{-5}$, both absolute, a ratio near $900$.
Shocks, current sheets and shear layers occupy few cells, so displacing one
feature slightly, or limiting the reconstruction differently beside it,
dominates $L_\infty$ while leaving the domain average alone.

The solver results sharpen this. HLL replaces the internal Riemann fan by a
diffusive two-wave approximation, whereas HLLD restores the intermediate waves
\citep{miyoshiKusano2005hlld} and carries 14 guarded degeneracy decisions with
an HLL fallback. Its non-zero branch response must originate at the three
intermediate ties, since HLL's own equality branches are unreachable by
construction rather than by luck
(Sections~\ref{sec:ch2-riemann} and \ref{sec:ch5-build-sensitivity}). The
lesson is one of counting rather than quality: exposure to an implementation
detail follows from how many internal decisions can be reached, not from
accuracy or dissipation, so the more accurate Riemann solver is here the more
implementation-sensitive one.

Build and device comparisons concern other mechanisms again. Null saved-state
differences for the covered CPU/GPU HLL pairs establish deterministic
equivalence under those matched operations and configurations, but that
equivalence was arranged rather than found: the device kernels were compiled
with multiply--add contraction disabled to match the host's non-contracting
default, against nvcc's opposite default (Section~\ref{sec:ch2-execution}).
Restoring that default removed the agreement in every pair, and on Brio--Wu
in fp32 it moved the density further than halving the precision did
(Section~\ref{sec:ch5-hardware}). Bitwise agreement across processor types is
therefore a property of the build configuration, not of the hardware, and
reproducing it elsewhere requires that one flag in the recorded recipe. The
instruction-set null result sharpens the same point: widening the registers
changed nothing, because the semantics, not the instruction mix, decide. The non-zero /fp:fast comparisons show relaxed
arithmetic semantics perturbing the computed trajectory; reassociation and
fused multiply--add contraction are among the transformations /fp:fast permits
\citep{microsoftMsvcFp2021}, and the measurements do not say which was active.

These distinctions also stop ``stable'' becoming shorthand for
``reproducible'': finite, positive completion over the tested horizon, a
changed saved field and an identical stored representation are three different
claims.

The temporal study is a statement about sampling. Both cases approach a common
level near 2.3 roundings in the density mean at the resolutions used, Brio--Wu
from about a tenth of a rounding and Orszag--Tang from just below it
(Section~\ref{sec:ch5-temporal}), so the expectation that a chaotic
two-dimensional problem must separate faster than a one-dimensional shock tube
is not tested here: the series differ in sampling window, not in mechanism. A
fitted growth rate of this kind means little without the level it approaches,
and extrapolated beyond its window it overstates the separation.

Performance is workload-dependent: in cell updates per second the CPU rate
barely moved between the two problems while the GPU rate spanned seventyfold,
which is what amortising fixed launch, transfer and output costs looks like.
Modest CPU precision speed-ups likewise need not track arithmetic throughput
when memory traffic and control flow share the runtime. That reading agrees
with the Roofline model \citep{williamsEtAl2009roofline}, but component
timings and hardware counters were not collected.

\section{Accuracy, discrepancy, and performance trade-offs}
\label{sec:ch6-tradeoffs}

Reference error, cross-variant discrepancy and wall time answer separate
questions. Only Brio--Wu has error against a named, non-exact reference; the
other pairs measure change from project baselines, and every reported field
norm is density-only.

\section{Reproducibility of a published algorithm}
\label{sec:ch6-published-algorithm}

Operationally the MUSCL--Hancock/GLM update with HLL or HLLD proved reliable:
across two precisions, four CFL numbers, three grids and two devices, every
run in the reported matrix finished with finite, positive states, the single
Orszag--Tang HLLD exception needing the first-order fallback of
Section~\ref{sec:ch4-orszag-tang}. Insensitivity is another matter, since
relaxed floating-point semantics and the HLLD branch rule each moved the saved
field, and the fp32--fp64 separation grew under refinement instead of
shrinking. Both hold at once: a scheme can be perfectly stable and still fail
to reproduce bit for bit across builds.

Reproducing a published algorithm therefore requires more than naming HLL,
HLLD or MUSCL--Hancock; terminology differs across fields, so the distinctions
here are operational \citep{plesser2018reproducibility}. The record must fix an
immutable source revision, a build recipe with the effective compiler and
floating-point options, a complete case definition, the algorithmic choices
that alter the arithmetic path, and the execution platform, acceptance criteria
and timing protocol actually used, bound to the retained artefacts by hash.
Section~\ref{sec:ch3-harness-metadata} sets out those layers
\citep{sandveEtAl2013reproducible} and
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-provenance} supplies them for this report;
containers preserve much of the software environment, but not
hardware-dependent arithmetic.

The record supports two distinct claims. Bitwise reproducibility requires the
same stored representation under tightly matched execution; scientific
reproducibility requires the declared conclusion to survive comparison with a
justified reference and stated tolerances, and can stand where bitwise
agreement between independent implementations is infeasible, provided
unexplained differences are exposed through retained outputs. Configuration
and metadata are part of the reported result.
