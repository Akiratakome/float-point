# Writing a Dissertation Conclusion

## Overview

A conclusion **synthesises**; it does not summarise. It answers the question from the introduction, states the contribution, calibrates how far it travels, and points to grounded next steps. Shape: an **inverted funnel** — specific findings, then broader implications, then take-home sentence.

Summary repeats *what was done*; synthesis says *what it means now that we have done it*.

## When to use

- Drafting the final chapter of a research dissertation or thesis
- Revising a draft reviewers call "descriptive" or "just a list of results"
- The aim from the introduction is not visibly answered at the end
- Limitations read as apology, or future work as filler

### When NOT to use

- Abstracts, discussion sections, or executive summaries (those have different conventions).

## Quick Reference: the six moves, in order

| # | Move | One-sentence test |
|---|------|--------------------|
| 1 | Restate the aim | Does the reader recognise the question from the introduction? |
| 2 | State key findings | Are the 2–4 load-bearing results named, not all results? |
| 3 | State implication / contribution | What is now known or doable that was not before? |
| 4 | Calibrate the claim | Are hedges placed where the evidence is thinnest? |
| 5 | Acknowledge limitations | Do they bound the claim without dissolving it? |
| 6 | Suggest concrete future work | Does each suggestion follow from a finding or limitation? |

## Core moves

1. **Restate, do not re-introduce.** One sentence echoing the introduction's terms. No new literature.
2. **Lead with load-bearing findings.** The 2–4 results without which the contribution collapses. Numbers live here.
3. **Move outward to implication.** State the contribution: a method, a calibrated trade-off, a falsified assumption, a usable rule of thumb.
4. **Calibrate before you defend.** Tentative verbs (*suggest, indicate*), modals (*may, can*), and scope qualifiers (*for the configurations tested*) belong on the implication, not on the finding. Findings are reported; implications are hedged.
5. **Limitations bound, they do not retract.** Name the specific boundary (one solver, one mesh family) and tie it to the part of the claim it constrains. Do not list every imperfection.
6. **Future work earns its place by being specific.** Each suggestion traces to a finding or limitation just stated.

## Common mistakes

- **Summary creep** — recapping methods. Fix: delete anything known by chapter 3.
- **Flat finding-list** — no synthesis. Fix: cluster findings under their implication.
- **Over-hedging** — "may possibly indicate that...". Fix: one hedge per claim.
- **Under-hedging the implication** — local finding, universal claim. Fix: add scope.
- **Apologetic limitations** — "Unfortunately, due to time...". Fix: state the bound neutrally.
- **Generic future work** — "further research is needed". Fix: name the next experiment.
- **No take-home sentence.** Fix: end with one declarative sentence a reader could quote.

## Worked example (annotated)

Model closing paragraph structure. Use it as a pattern for moves, not as reusable content.

> **[Restate aim]** This study examined how a defined technical choice affects a measurable outcome in a controlled setting. **[Key finding 1]** The first main result establishes the central empirical pattern and names the evidence supporting it. **[Key finding 2]** The second main result explains a mechanism, contrast, or boundary condition that changes how the first result should be interpreted. **[Implication / contribution]** Together these findings suggest a limited but useful conclusion about the system under study. **[Limitation, calibrated]** This conclusion applies to the cases examined and should not be extended to untested regimes without further evidence. **[Grounded future work]** The next study should test the weakest boundary identified above, using a method that can distinguish between the remaining explanations.

Notice: findings carry numbers; the implication carries the broader hedge (*suggest*); the limitation bounds without retracting; future work points at one specific experiment per clause; the paragraph closes forward, not backward.
