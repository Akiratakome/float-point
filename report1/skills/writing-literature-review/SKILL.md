# Writing a Literature Review

## Overview

A literature review is **not a chronological summary of papers**. It is a critical, selective narrative that (a) demonstrates command of the field, (b) evaluates prior work, and (c) motivates the gap the dissertation fills. Cambridge ELO's headline failure mode: reviews that are "too inclusive and insufficiently related" to the candidate's own research.

Per theme: characterise an approach, acknowledge its strength, expose its limitation, link that limitation to your research need. Synthesise into a gap statement.

## When to Use

- Drafting the literature review chapter or its portion of the introduction
- Revising a draft that reads like a list ("Smith did X. Jones did Y.")
- Choosing a reporting verb and unsure whether it commits you to the cited claim
- Writing the synthesis paragraph that bridges into your own contribution

## Quick Reference: Reporting Verbs by Stance

Choose the verb that signals your distance from the source's claim.

| Stance | Verbs | Use when |
|--------|-------|----------|
| **Neutral / descriptive** | describe, present, report, examine, investigate, develop, model | Stating what a study did without committing yourself |
| **Endorsing (you accept)** | show, demonstrate, establish, prove, find, reveal | Claim is well-supported and you build on it |
| **Hedged / tentative** | suggest, indicate, imply, point to, appear to show | Evidence is partial or you want to soften commitment |
| **Author-attributed (their view)** | argue, claim, contend, propose, advocate, maintain, posit | Source is making an interpretive case you have not endorsed |
| **Critical / limiting** | overlook, neglect, assume, restrict, fail to account for, be limited to | Setting up the gap your work fills |

Hedges: see the hedging ladder in [`academic-english-style`](../academic-english-style/SKILL.md).
Soft critique: *purely, rather pragmatic, ad hoc, primarily focused on...*

## Citation Form

- **Integral** ("Author (Year) demonstrates...") — when the author's stance matters or you critique them specifically.
- **Non-integral** ("The method preserves the target invariant under these conditions (Author, Year).") — when grouping sources or when the claim, not the author, is the point.

## Core Moves (per paragraph)

1. **Open** with the theme or sub-problem, not an author name.
2. **Characterise** the approach: one representative source described, others listed.
3. **Acknowledge** strengths so your critique is fair.
4. **Expose** a specific limitation, linked to your research need.
5. **Close** with the consequence — what this prevents, what your work addresses.

The final paragraph of the chapter collates limitations into one explicit **gap statement** bridging to your contribution.

## Worked Example (structure only)

> **[1 Theme]** A technical field often treats one modelling assumption as routine, even though that assumption can affect the reliability of the results. **[2 Baseline]** Foundational studies establish the standard method and explain why it became widely adopted. **[3 Consensus]** Later studies extend the method and report acceptable performance under conventional evaluation conditions. **[4 Limitation]** However, this body of work tends to assume that one source of uncertainty is negligible, and therefore does not isolate its effect on the reported results. **[5 Opportunity]** A more targeted measurement approach can test this assumption directly. **[6 Gap]** The present study addresses this gap by measuring the effect of that uncertainty on the method's accuracy and stability.

**Move annotations:**
- [1] Opens with the theme, not an author.
- [2] Endorsing verb (*establish*) signals that you accept the baseline rather than merely report it.
- [3] Neutral verbs (*extend, report*) group later sources around a shared position without committing to their interpretation.
- [4] Pivot via *however*; *tends to assume* is a critical/limiting move that names a specific weakness without attacking the cited authors.
- [5] Hedged verb (*can test*) signals an opportunity rather than a confirmed result.
- [6] Gap closes with the dissertation's specific contribution, mirroring the limitation exposed in [4].

Do not copy the example as prose. Replace each move with verified sources, accurate claims, and the specific gap your dissertation addresses.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Chronological dump ("In 1959... In 1978... In 2009...") | Organise thematically; chronology only within a theme when it matters |
| One paragraph per paper | One paragraph per **sub-problem**; group papers that share an approach |
| Flat verbs everywhere (*says, talks about, mentions*) | Vary by stance using the table above |
| Endorsing a contested claim by accident (*Higham shows that...* when you mean *argues*) | Use author-attributed verbs (*argue, claim, contend*) for interpretive claims |
| Listing limitations without linking to your work | End each critical paragraph with the consequence for your research |
| Missing gap statement | Final paragraph must collate limitations into one explicit gap and bridge to your contribution |
| Too inclusive | If a paper does not connect to your gap, cut it or list it in a grouped cite |
