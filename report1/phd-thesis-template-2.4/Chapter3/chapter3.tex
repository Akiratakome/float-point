\chapter{Numerical Method}

This chapter fixes the finite-volume MUSCL--Hancock method used in Chapter~5.
Chapter~4 then varies precision, backend, compiler flags, and selected branch
rules around this fixed update.

\section{Finite-Volume Update}
% <<SECTION_1_BEGIN>>
For the ideal-gas Euler system the conserved state is
\(U=(\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E)^T\). In two dimensions it satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-euler-2d}
  U_t + F(U)_x + G(U)_y = 0,
\end{equation}
with physical fluxes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-euler-fluxes}
  F(U)=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \rho u\\ \rho u^2+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(E+p)
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  G(U)=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2+p\\ v(E+p)
  \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
On the Cartesian cell
\(C_{ij}=[x_{i-1/2},x_{i+1/2}]\times[y_{j-1/2},y_{j+1/2}]\), integrating over
\([t^n,t^{n+1}]\times C_{ij}\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-fv-integral}
\begin{aligned}
  &\int_{C_{ij}} U(x,y,t^{n+1})\,dx\,dy
  - \int_{C_{ij}} U(x,y,t^n)\,dx\,dy \\
  &\quad
  + \int_{t^n}^{t^{n+1}}\!\!\int_{y_{j-1/2}}^{y_{j+1/2}}
      \left[F(U(x_{i+1/2},y,t))-F(U(x_{i-1/2},y,t))\right]\,dy\,dt \\
  &\quad
  + \int_{t^n}^{t^{n+1}}\!\!\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}
      \left[G(U(x,y_{j+1/2},t))-G(U(x,y_{j-1/2},t))\right]\,dx\,dt
  =0 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The discrete unknown is the cell average
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-cell-average}
  \bar U_{ij}^{\,n}
  = \frac{1}{\Delta x\,\Delta y}
    \int_{C_{ij}} U(x,y,t^n)\,dx\,dy .
\end{equation}
The interface flux is the time- and face-averaged physical flux, approximated in
practice by a Riemann solver:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-interface-flux}
  \widehat F_{i+1/2,j}^{\,n}
  =
  \frac{1}{\Delta t^n\,\Delta y}
  \int_{t^n}^{t^{n+1}}\!\!\int_{y_{j-1/2}}^{y_{j+1/2}}
  F(U(x_{i+1/2},y,t))\,dy\,dt ,
\end{equation}
with \(\widehat G_{i,j+1/2}^{\,n}\) defined analogously on horizontal faces.
Substitution gives the conservative flux-difference update
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fv-update}
  \bar U_{ij}^{\,n+1}
  =
  \bar U_{ij}^{\,n}
  - \frac{\Delta t^n}{\Delta x}
    \left(\widehat F_{i+1/2,j}^{\,n}
          -\widehat F_{i-1/2,j}^{\,n}\right)
  - \frac{\Delta t^n}{\Delta y}
    \left(\widehat G_{i,j+1/2}^{\,n}
          -\widehat G_{i,j-1/2}^{\,n}\right).
\end{equation}

Fig.~\ref{fig:ch3-fv-cell} sketches the four interface fluxes in this update.
A shared face therefore contributes equal and opposite fluxes to neighbouring
cells, so summing the update cancels all interior exchanges and leaves only
boundary fluxes. This cancellation is the finite-volume conservation mechanism
used below, following the standard treatments of \citet{toro2009} and
\citet{leveque_2002}. The explicit time step is controlled by a
Courant--Friedrichs--Lewy (CFL) condition on local acoustic wave speeds; the
full formula is given in Section~\ref{sec:ch3-stability}.
Equation~\ref{eq:fv-update} fixes the
two-direction notation; the implementation does not advance it as an unsplit
transverse-flux scheme. Instead, Chapter~4 uses alternating directional sweeps:
each two-dimensional step is built from one-dimensional conservative updates in
\(x\) and \(y\), with alternating order to reduce fixed ordering bias.

\section{MUSCL-Hancock Reconstruction and Predictor Step}
% <<SECTION_2_BEGIN>>
\paragraph{Slope limiting.}
The MUSCL-Hancock step uses a piecewise-linear reconstruction inside each cell,
followed by a half-time predictor before the Riemann solve. Near shocks and
contacts an unlimited piecewise-linear reconstruction can introduce new local
extrema and spurious oscillations. A slope limiter suppresses these extrema
while retaining second-order accuracy in smooth monotone regions. The
reconstruction follows the MUSCL framework of van~Leer~\citep{vanleer_1979}, in
the presentation of \citet{toro2009}. The reconstruction uses one-sided jumps
and Toro's minbee limiter function \(\Phi(r)\). For the jump orientation used
by the code this gives the following component-wise limited jump:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-minmod-sigma}
  \sigma_i^{(k)}
  =
  \operatorname{minmod}\!\left(\bar U_i^{(k),n}-\bar U_{i-1}^{(k),n},\;
                               \bar U_{i+1}^{(k),n}-\bar U_i^{(k),n}\right),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-minmod-def}
  \operatorname{minmod}(a,b)
  =
  \begin{cases}
    0, & ab\le 0,\\
    \operatorname{sgn}(a)\min\!\left(|a|,|b|\right), & ab>0 .
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, writing the one-sided jumps as
\(\delta^- U_i^{(k)} = \bar U_i^{(k),n}-\bar U_{i-1}^{(k),n}\) and
\(\delta^+ U_i^{(k)} = \bar U_{i+1}^{(k),n}-\bar U_i^{(k),n}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-minbee-function}
  \sigma_i^{(k)} = \Phi(r_i^{(k)})\,\delta^+ U_i^{(k)},\qquad
  \Phi(r) = \max(0, \min(1, r)),\qquad
  r_i^{(k)} = \frac{\delta^- U_i^{(k)}}{\delta^+ U_i^{(k)}} .
\end{equation}
\(\Phi\) is Toro's minbee function; Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch3-minmod-sigma} is its
limited-jump implementation here, not a claim that all minbee and minmod
limiter forms are interchangeable.
Minbee is a diffusive Total Variation Diminishing (TVD) limiter, so small
perturbations may be damped. For a scalar conservation law a TVD scheme cannot
increase the discrete total variation in time and so cannot generate spurious
extrema; for the nonlinear Euler system this property is heuristic rather than
proved. The quantity \(\sigma_i\) is therefore a component-wise limited cell
jump used in the linear reconstruction, not a derivative \(dU/dx\).

\paragraph{Hancock predictor.}
The reconstructed states at the left and right faces of cell \(i\) are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-reconstructed-states}
  U_{i,L}^{n}=\bar U_i^{n}-\frac12\sigma_i,
  \qquad
  U_{i,R}^{n}=\bar U_i^{n}+\frac12\sigma_i .
\end{equation}
A local Hancock half-step then advances them using the flux difference across
the reconstructed cell profile:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-hancock-half-step-L}
  \bar U_{i,L}^{\,n+1/2}
  =
  U_{i,L}^{n}
  -\frac{\Delta t}{2\Delta x}
  \left[F(U_{i,R}^{n})-F(U_{i,L}^{n})\right],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-hancock-half-step-R}
  \bar U_{i,R}^{\,n+1/2}
  =
  U_{i,R}^{n}
  -\frac{\Delta t}{2\Delta x}
  \left[F(U_{i,R}^{n})-F(U_{i,L}^{n})\right].
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Riemann input.}
The Riemann problem at \(x_{i+1/2}\) is finally posed from neighbouring
predicted states,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-riemann-input}
  U_{i+1/2}^{L}=\bar U_{i,R}^{\,n+1/2},
  \qquad
  U_{i+1/2}^{R}=\bar U_{i+1,L}^{\,n+1/2},
  \qquad
  \widehat F_{i+1/2}
  =
  \mathcal{R}\!\left(U_{i+1/2}^{L},U_{i+1/2}^{R}\right).
\end{equation}
The construction applies identically in the \(y\)-direction.

\paragraph{Predecessors.}
Godunov's first-order method advances piecewise-constant data through a
Riemann-solver flux \citep{toro2009},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-godunov}
  U_i^{n+1}
  = U_i^n
  - \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\bigl[\widehat F(U_i,U_{i+1}) - \widehat F(U_{i-1},U_i)\bigr],
\end{equation}
but the constant-state assumption caps accuracy at first order and smears
contacts over \(\mathcal{O}(\Delta x)\) cells. The Lax--Wendroff scheme reaches
second order through a centred update \citep{toro2009},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-lax-wendroff}
  U_i^{n+1}
  = U_i^n
  - \frac{\Delta t}{2\Delta x}(F_{i+1}-F_{i-1})
  + \frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2\Delta x^{2}}\bigl[A_{i+1/2}(F_{i+1}-F_i) - A_{i-1/2}(F_i-F_{i-1})\bigr],
\end{equation}
with \(A=\partial F/\partial U\), yet without a limiter it oscillates near
discontinuities. MUSCL--Hancock combines limited piecewise-linear
reconstruction (replacing Godunov's constants) with a Riemann-solver flux
(replacing LW's centred average), securing second-order smooth accuracy and
TVD behaviour at discontinuities \citep{vanleer_1979}.

\section{HLLC and Rusanov Fluxes}
% <<SECTION_3_BEGIN>>
At an interface the predictor supplies left and right states \(U_L\) and
\(U_R\), so the local problem is the one-dimensional Riemann problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-riemann-init}
  U_{\mathrm{RP}}(x,0)=
  \begin{cases}
    U_L, & x<0,\\
    U_R, & x>0 .
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The report's main validation flux is HLLC. In the construction introduced by
\citet{toro_spruce_speares_1994} and presented in \citet{toro2009}, this flux
approximates the fan by left, contact, and right waves with speeds \(S_L\),
\(S_\ast\), and \(S_R\). In the wave-speed bound of \citet{davis_1988} as presented by
\citet{toro2009},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-hllc-wavespeeds}
  S_L=\min(u_L-a_L,u_R-a_R), \qquad
  S_R=\max(u_L+a_L,u_R+a_R),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-hllc-sstar}
  S_\ast =
  \frac{p_R-p_L+\rho_L u_L(S_L-u_L)-\rho_R u_R(S_R-u_R)}
       {\rho_L(S_L-u_L)-\rho_R(S_R-u_R)} .
\end{equation}

The approximate solution sampled at \(\xi=x/t\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-hllc-sampled}
  \tilde U(\xi)=
  \begin{cases}
    U_L, & \xi<S_L,\\
    U_{\ast L}, & S_L<\xi<S_\ast,\\
    U_{\ast R}, & S_\ast<\xi<S_R,\\
    U_R, & S_R<\xi .
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The corresponding interface flux is selected by the sign of the wave speeds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-hllc-fluxselect}
  \widehat F_{\mathrm{HLLC}}=
  \begin{cases}
    F_L, & S_L\ge 0,\\
    F_{\ast L}, & S_L<0\le S_\ast,\\
    F_{\ast R}, & S_\ast<0\le S_R,\\
    F_R, & S_R<0 .
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For \(K\in\{L,R\}\), the star-region flux is written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-hllc-fstark}
  F_{\ast K}=F_K+S_K\left(U_{\ast K}-U_K\right),
\end{equation}
where \(U_{\ast K}\) is obtained from the Rankine--Hugoniot jump conditions
across \(S_K\) and may be written in closed form as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-hllc-ustark}
  U_{\ast K}
  =
  \rho_K\,\frac{S_K-u_K}{S_K-S_\ast}
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1\\[2pt]
    S_\ast\\[2pt]
    v_K\\[2pt]
    \dfrac{E_K}{\rho_K}
    +(S_\ast-u_K)\!\left[S_\ast+\dfrac{p_K}{\rho_K(S_K-u_K)}\right]
  \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
The scalar prefactor multiplies the whole vector; the fourth entry has units of energy density once the prefactor is included; the bracketed factor is total energy per unit mass plus the contact-pressure correction. The contact pressure
\(p_\ast=p_K+\rho_K(S_K-u_K)(S_\ast-u_K)\) is equal on both sides of the contact.
This is the part of HLLC that restores the contact wave missing from the
two-wave HLL approximation discussed in the literature
\citep{toro_spruce_speares_1994,batten_etal_1997,toro2009}. The exact choice of strict or non-strict
inequality at zero is a precision-sensitive implementation decision and is
discussed separately in Section~\ref{sec:precision-sensitive-decisions}.

Rusanov, also called the local Lax--Friedrichs flux, is used later as a
controlled method comparator, not as the main validation method or as an
accuracy recommendation. It changes only the
interface flux inside the same MUSCL--Hancock update, leaving reconstruction and
time integration fixed, so the comparison isolates the contact-wave, branch, and
arithmetic path introduced by HLLC. The flux replaces the contact-resolving HLLC
structure by a centred flux plus scalar dissipation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-rusanov}
  \widehat F_{\mathrm{Rus}}
  =
  \frac12\left(F_L+F_R\right)
  -\frac12 S_{\max}\left(U_R-U_L\right),
  \qquad
  S_{\max}=\max\left(|u_L|+a_L,\ |u_R|+a_R\right).
\end{equation}
Rusanov has no contact-wave branch and stronger numerical diffusion than HLLC \citep{harten_lax_vanleer_1983,toro2009}, which makes the HLLC/Rusanov comparison in Chapter~5 an isolation of the contact-resolving path rather than an accuracy ranking.

\section{Stability, Limiting, and Positivity}
% <<SECTION_4_BEGIN>>
\label{sec:ch3-stability}
Stability is controlled by nondimensional Courant numbers rather than by the
time step alone, with the relevant one-cell signal speeds being the acoustic
bounds \(|u|+c\) and \(|v|+c\) in each coordinate direction. The implemented
update therefore selects the time step globally from the local wave speeds,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-cfl-timestep}
  \Delta t^{n}
  =
  C_{\mathrm{CFL}}\,
  \min_{ij}\!\left(
    \frac{\Delta x}{|u_{ij}|+c_{ij}},\
    \frac{\Delta y}{|v_{ij}|+c_{ij}}
  \right),
  \qquad
  0<C_{\mathrm{CFL}}\le 1,
\end{equation}
where \(c_{ij}\) is the sound speed in cell \(ij\). The associated per-cell
Courant numbers in the two coordinate directions are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-courant-numbers}
  \nu_{x,ij}
  =
  \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\left(|u_{ij}|+c_{ij}\right),
  \qquad
  \nu_{y,ij}
  =
  \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}\left(|v_{ij}|+c_{ij}\right).
\end{equation}
Substituting Equation~\ref{eq:ch3-cfl-timestep} into
Equation~\ref{eq:ch3-courant-numbers} gives
\(\max_{ij}\max(\nu_{x,ij},\nu_{y,ij})\le C_{\mathrm{CFL}}\) directly, so the
formula is the split-update per-direction restriction. The implementation then
applies Equation~\ref{eq:fv-update} as successive one-dimensional sweeps in
\(x\) and \(y\); \(C_{\mathrm{CFL}}\) is a local acoustic wave-speed safety
factor, not a universal stability constant \citep{toro2009,leveque_2002}. This
report does not use the split form to claim a global unsplit
or Strang-split second-order-in-time theorem; Chapter~5 demonstrates observed
two-dimensional benchmark agreement against numerical references instead.

For a scalar monotone first-order scheme, a TVD argument gives a direct
Courant-number restriction. MUSCL-Hancock uses second-order reconstruction and a
predictor step, then relies on limiting to suppress new oscillations near
nonsmooth data. The limiter makes the reconstruction nonlinear, so the local
order is not uniform across the grid. In smooth monotone regions the
piecewise-linear data and Hancock predictor give the expected second-order
truncation behaviour. At extrema, shocks, contacts, and rarefaction edges the
minbee limiter, implemented by Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch3-minmod-sigma}, can set or reduce the slope,
moving the local update towards a first-order Godunov step
\citep{vanleer_1979,toro2009}.

The same restriction is also tied to physical admissibility. The primitive
conversion requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-positivity}
  \rho > 0,
  \qquad
  p=(\gamma-1)\left(E-\frac12\rho(u^2+v^2)\right)>0 .
\end{equation}
Large Courant numbers, strong waves, or near-vacuum states can produce predicted
or updated states outside this admissible set even when the conservative update
is algebraically well defined. HLLC wave-speed estimates and limiter decisions
therefore act together with the CFL choice: they determine not only linear
stability, but also whether the intermediate states used in the Riemann solve
remain physically meaningful. Batten-type HLLC wave-speed choices support
robustness and admissibility \citep{batten_etal_1997}, but no explicit
positivity limiter or general positivity proof is claimed here; the CFL and
limiter choices are treated as part of the controlled numerical method.
% <<SECTION_4_END>>

\section{Precision-Sensitive Decision Points}
\label{sec:precision-sensitive-decisions}
% <<SECTION_5_BEGIN>>
\paragraph{Rounding model.}
A useful local model for the floating-point part of the method is the standard
one-operation rounding model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-rounding-model}
  \operatorname{fl}(a \mathbin{\mathrm{op}} b)
  =
  (a \mathbin{\mathrm{op}} b)(1+\delta),
  \qquad |\delta|\le u ,
\end{equation}
for a basic arithmetic operation under round-to-nearest-even IEEE\,754 semantics,
away from overflow, underflow, and exceptional cases
\citep{ieee754_2019,goldberg_1991,higham_2002}. A flux evaluation is a composed
map: primitive recovery, sound-speed evaluation, wave-speed estimates, star-state
construction, and flux differences each introduce small perturbations. Some
intermediate quantities are sufficiently accurate in binary64 but insufficiently
so in binary32, and HLLC comparisons mean rounded operands can change which
algebraic formula is sampled at an interface. FMA contraction can change two
rounded operations into one fused rounded operation; compiler and device flags
determine whether contraction occurs \citep{ieee754_2019,higham_2002}.

\paragraph{Primitive recovery.}
Recovered pressure follows
\(p = (\gamma-1)\bigl(E - \tfrac{1}{2}\rho(u^2+v^2)\bigr)\),
and the subtraction \(E - \tfrac{1}{2}\rho\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert^{2}\) is the
cancellation site: when kinetic energy approaches total energy the two operands
are of like sign and comparable magnitude, violating the Sterbenz condition and
shedding leading digits in the difference \citep{higham_2002}. This regime is
reached in the high-Mach pockets of LW12, in particular the upper-right
shock-interaction arc highlighted in Fig.~\ref{fig:ch5-lw12-precision-heatmap},
and in the colliding-shock star state of Toro5. The recovered pressure feeds
the sound speed \(a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}\), which in turn enters the HLLC
wave-speed estimates (Eq.~\ref{eq:ch3-hllc-wavespeeds}) and the contact speed
\(S_\ast\) (Eq.~\ref{eq:ch3-hllc-sstar}). In fp32 the relative accuracy of \(p\)
in these regions is reduced by roughly the cancellation factor, and that loss
propagates into \(\widehat S_L,\widehat S_\ast,\widehat S_R\), amplifying the
branch sensitivity discussed next.

\paragraph{Branch sensitivity.}
For HLLC the branch decision uses computed estimates
\(\widehat S_L,\widehat S_\ast,\widehat S_R\), not exact wave speeds. The
logical test
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-branch-test}
  S_L \le 0 \le S_\ast
  \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
  S_L < 0 < S_\ast
\end{equation}
may only affect results when one of the computed wave speeds is very close to
zero. Exact arithmetic distinguishes the two versions only at equality, but the
computed sign of \(\widehat S_\ast\) is a quotient,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-sstar-decomp}
  S_\ast=\frac{N_\ast}{D_\ast}, \qquad
  D_\ast=\rho_L(S_L-u_L)-\rho_R(S_R-u_R),
\end{equation}
and a first-order perturbation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-sstar-perturbation}
  \frac{\Delta S_\ast}{S_\ast}
  \approx
  \frac{\Delta N_\ast}{N_\ast}
  -
  \frac{\Delta D_\ast}{D_\ast},
\end{equation}
when the terms are non-zero. The numerator \(N_\ast\) contains the pressure
jump and two momentum-wave terms, so nearly balanced states can lose leading
digits before division by \(D_\ast\). The denominator has the same risk when
\(\rho_L(S_L-u_L)\) and \(\rho_R(S_R-u_R)\) nearly balance. Cancellation in
\(N_\ast\) or \(D_\ast\) can amplify rounded changes in the wave-speed estimate,
and the amplified value may then enter a discontinuous branch selection
\citep{toro2009}. When $N_\ast = 0$ exactly by symmetry (the degenerate case examined in Chapter 5 Section \emph{Toro Test 2 Branch Stability}), the relative perturbation in Equation~\ref{eq:ch3-sstar-perturbation} is undefined and the sign of the computed $S_\ast$ is set by accumulated rounding noise rather than by first-order linearisation.

\section{Extension to Ideal MHD}
% <<SECTION_6_BEGIN>>
Report~1 uses Euler, the hydrodynamic limit of the later ideal-MHD problem. In
ideal MHD the conserved state augments \((\rho,\rho\mathbf{u},E)\) with
\(\mathbf{B}\), adding magnetic pressure, tension, and induction terms. Its
Riemann fan has seven families: two fast magnetosonic, two Alfven, two slow
magnetosonic, and one entropy/contact wave, making it more complex than Euler's
three-wave HLLC fan \citep{toro2009}. With normal velocity \(u_n\) and fast,
Alfven, slow speeds \(c_f, c_a, c_s\), the seven characteristic speeds are
\(u_n-c_f,\ u_n-c_a,\ u_n-c_s,\ u_n,\ u_n+c_s,\ u_n+c_a,\ u_n+c_f\); see
Fig.~\ref{fig:ch3-mhd-seven-wave-fan}.

The magnetic field must satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-divB-zero}
  \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0,
\end{equation}
at the continuous level, but a finite-volume update does not enforce this
constraint automatically; discrete divergence can create spurious magnetic
forces and distort wave speeds, motivating the divergence-control strategies
surveyed in the literature \citep{toth_2000}.

Dedner et al.'s hyperbolic cleaning approach \citep{dedner_2002} augments the
induction equation with a scalar field \(\psi\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch3-dedner-cleaning}
  \partial_t \mathbf{B}
  + \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u})
  + \nabla\psi = 0,
  \qquad
  \partial_t \psi + c_h^2\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = -\psi/\tau .
\end{equation}
Here \(\psi\) carries divergence error, \(c_h\) is the cleaning-wave speed, and
\(\tau=c_p^2/c_h^2\) is the damping time, so \(-\psi/\tau\) matches
\(-(c_h^2/c_p^2)\psi\) in Dedner's parameterisation. Constrained transport \citep{evans_hawley_1988}
instead preserves a staggered-mesh discrete divergence constraint. This is also
why the Euler HLLC flux is not a direct full-MHD choice: its three-wave
acoustic/contact model does not resolve the fast, Alfven, slow, and contact
families, nor the discrete divergence constraint. HLL gives a simpler but more
diffusive bound \citep{harten_lax_vanleer_1983}; HLLD \citep{miyoshi_kusano_2005}
and Roe-type solvers \citep{roe_1981} aim to retain more MHD wave structure,
while GLM-Dedner cleaning and constrained transport treat divergence error
differently. Report~1 does not derive an MHD finite-volume update, discrete GLM
source treatment, HLLD star states, or a divergence diagnostic; those are
Report~2 implementation and validation tasks.
Report~1 evidence remains Euler-only. The added magnetic pressure terms and
near-degenerate wave speeds provide more branch and cancellation sites than
Euler, so they require separate validation.
