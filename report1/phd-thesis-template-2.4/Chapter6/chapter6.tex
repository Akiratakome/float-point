\section{Precision Adequacy and Region-Aware Diagnostics}
% <<SECTION_2_BEGIN>>
Exact Riemann and high-resolution numerical references enter through Chapter~4 Equation~\eqref{eq:ch4-rref}, while matched CPU/GPU runs test saved outputs under a fixed within-case setup. For non-degenerate comparisons, direct IEEE fp32/fp64 final-state differences stay below the available reference or discretisation scale in both density and pressure. The LW12 upper-right localisation suggests sensitivity where reconstructed states feed HLLC wave-speed or flux decisions, without proving that a specific branch changed. The stationary-contact infinite ratio is degenerate (zero denominator). These diagnostics locate precision pressure rather than introduce a separate IEEE-fp32 claim.

The MCA toolchain is defined in Chapter~2 Section~\emph{Verificarlo and Monte Carlo Arithmetic}; the $n = 30$ seed sampling follows Chapter~4 Section~\emph{Precision and Hardware Variants}. The same ensemble idea is read off a one-dimensional Sod diagnostic before extending to LW3. Figure~\ref{fig:ch6-sigma-fp} then uses the \(\sigma_{\mathrm{FP}}\) local roundoff scale of Chapter~4 Section~4.3: for both HLLC and Rusanov, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{FP}}\) drops as virtual mantissa precision rises. Lower or similar Rusanov values pick up the combined effect of a different arithmetic path and extra diffusion smoothing gradients. They are not an accuracy ranking or a reproducibility drift. The virtual-precision labels are diagnostic mantissa settings rather than IEEE storage formats. The \emph{loss-of-significance score} (LoSoS) for a primitive component $q$ at cell $\mathbf{x}$ is $\mathrm{LoSoS}(\mathbf{x}) = -\log_{10}\!\bigl(\sigma_{\mathrm{FP},q}(\mathbf{x})/|q(\mathbf{x})|\bigr)$. This counts the base-10 digits of $q$ that remain significant under the MCA noise scale $\sigma_{\mathrm{FP},q}$ defined in Chapter~4 Section~\emph{Precision and Hardware Variants} \citep{sohier_etal_2021}.

The loss-of-significance score LoSoS gives a complementary view of the same LW3 density samples. Figure~\ref{fig:ch6-losos-quantiles} reports sample quantiles rather than the worst cell. For HLLC the median LoSoS rises with virtual precision, while lower quantiles stay closer to the front-sensitive part of the field (Fig.~\ref{fig:ch6-losos-quantiles}). These quantiles are distributional estimates within the $n=30$ regime defined in Chapter~4 Section~\emph{Precision and Hardware Variants}.

The required-significant-digits threshold \(s_{\mathrm{req}}\) --- the number of significant digits a downstream consumer needs at the chosen reference-error scale --- turns LoSoS into a regional margin.
Figure~\ref{fig:ch6-region-losos} plots available digits at \texttt{p32} minus \(s_{\mathrm{req}}\). For HLLC at \texttt{p32}, the smooth interior keeps a positive margin while the shock-front band is negative; the transition/contact band sits in between (Fig.~\ref{fig:ch6-region-losos}). The noise-to-reference-error diagnostic in Fig.~\ref{fig:ch6-noise-error} gives a scale-normalised view. For both HLLC and Rusanov, the LW3 precision-limited cell fraction drops monotonically with virtual precision and reaches $0\,\%$ at \texttt{p32}. By this diagnostic, virtual \texttt{p32} MCA noise sits below the chosen reference-error threshold for LW3 at this resolution; this is not an IEEE fp32 adequacy claim. This reading is consistent with the Chapter~5 reference-scaled evidence but remains bounded to the tested precisions, cases, and the LW3 MCA grid. The \texttt{p8} curves are low-precision stress diagnostics; the final-time caveat is listed in Section~\ref{sec:ch6-limits}.

\paragraph{Ratio framing.}
The $\sigma_{\mathrm{FP}}$, LoSoS, $s_{\mathrm{req}}$, and ratio views use the local noise-to-reference-error convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch6-eta}
  \eta(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{FP}}(\mathbf{x})}{|U_{\mathrm{fp64}}(\mathbf{x}) - U_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{x})|},
\end{equation}
so Fig.~\ref{fig:ch6-region-snr} reports \(\eta\) by smooth, transition, and shock-front regions. Cells with \(\eta>1\) are precision-limited. Their fraction shrinks monotonically from \texttt{p8} to \texttt{p32}~\citep{sohier_etal_2021}.

\section{Hardware and Implementation Sensitivity}
% <<SECTION_3_BEGIN>>
The matched strict-IEEE HLLC CPU/GPU evidence (Table~\ref{tab:ch5-cpu-gpu}) is a saved-output statement, with zero drift across all five cases on both saved final states and saved checkpoints. The matched table does not establish equality of all intermediate stage values inside a time step, or equivalence across arbitrary compilers, devices, and build controls. The CSC strict-IEEE CPU-only vs CUDA-enabled \texttt{device=cpu} sanity check (Chapter~5 Section~\emph{Matched CPU/GPU Comparison}) further confirms that the CPU branch is not a CUDA-binary artefact under the tested strict cases.

The other axes show implementation or method sensitivity rather than a general hardware effect. In CPU double, the largest non-stationary compiler gap is O2 versus Ofast+fast-math for Toro3 and Toro5. Branch-rule entries test an implementation choice whose effect is smaller or absent in completed comparisons, so the branch-rule axis is method variation. The fp32 compiler matrix is precision sensitivity under compiler flags, not hardware evidence. HLLC versus Rusanov, limiter variation, and companion method checks support interpretation but sit outside the five-case validation claim. The GPU flag probe bounds the zero-drift result: changing strict versus fast CUDA controls can change saved GPU states. Checkpoint histories show finite-time, case-dependent drift growth, supporting a bounded time-window reading rather than an extrapolated rate.

\section{Limitations and Report 2 Direction}
\label{sec:ch6-limits}
% <<SECTION_4_BEGIN>>
The scope caveats are evidence-specific:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item LW12 is paired with an \(800^2\) fp64 numerical reference and a
        \(400^2 \to 800^2 \to 1600^2\) self-convergence hierarchy with slow
        \(L_1\) order \(\approx 0.535\); \(R_\rho\) magnitudes should be
        interpreted against this rate rather than as exact-solution agreement.
        LW3 retains the \(400^2/800^2/1600^2\) fp64 self-convergence
        reference.
  \item Compiler evidence uses one compiler family inside each case: GCC~13
        for the Linux/WSL cases; the Toro3 and Toro5 saved-state evidence in
        Chapter~5 now comes from the CSC Linux strict-IEEE rerun, with the
        earlier MSVC Build Tools build retained only as a historical
        comparator. No cross-compiler matrix is claimed.
  \item Hardware evidence uses the available local i9-13900H/RTX 4060 Laptop
        GPU and CSC AMD EPYC/RTX 5090 paths as bounded cross-toolchain/hardware
        evidence; portable behavior across arbitrary devices is not claimed.
  \item Verificarlo \texttt{p32} is a virtual mantissa precision, distinct
        from IEEE binary32; the $n=30$ MCA diagnostics support the
        \(p=90\%\), \(c=95.8\%\) sample-count reading stated in Chapter~4, not a
        \(p=99\%\), \(c=95\%\) claim. \texttt{p8} is a low-precision stress
        diagnostic only.
  \item Toro Test 2 with the strict \(<\) HLLC branch is a single-case
        diagnostic: the first-step equality fall-through is logged, but no
        systematic branch-rule study is claimed.
\end{enumerate}

Report~2 will extend the Chapter~5 drift-time-series to the MHD test set.
There chaotic dynamics motivate Lyapunov-type long-time separation
estimates rather than finite-time drift only. Dedner-type cleaning
\citep{dedner_2002} or constrained transport \citep{evans_hawley_1988}
and the HLLD scheme \citep{miyoshi_kusano_2005} are the candidate
MHD-side choices.
