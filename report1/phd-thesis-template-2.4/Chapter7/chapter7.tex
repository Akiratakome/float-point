\chapter{Conclusion}

\section{Aim and Evidence Base}
% <<SECTION_1_BEGIN>>
The central question was how precision and hardware/backend choices affect a Riemann-solver-based HRSC method on a controlled Euler validation suite. Evidence came from one- and two-dimensional Euler cases, matched CPU/GPU saved-output comparisons, compiler and branch-rule variations, and region-aware precision diagnostics.
% <<SECTION_1_END>>

\section{Key Findings}
% <<SECTION_2_BEGIN>>
Matched strict-HLLC CPU/GPU saved outputs were bit-identical within each tested case on its Windows BuildTools or Linux/WSL toolchain, with zero \(L_1\), \(L_\infty\), and \(\mathrm{ULP}_{\max}\); checkpoint equality applies only to saved states. Implementation sensitivity was axis-dependent: O2/O3 comparisons were bit-identical where tested, fast-math changed non-stationary saved states, and HLLC--Rusanov was deliberate method variation. Direct fp32/fp64 gaps stayed below the available reference scale; for LW12 at \(400^2\), the density ratio was \(1.30\times10^{-4}\), while Verificarlo \texttt{p32}, a virtual rather than IEEE fp32 mode, located remaining precision pressure on LW3 fronts. Together these findings provide a bounded Euler baseline for the later precision and hardware study.
% <<SECTION_2_END>>

\section{Limitation and Next Step}
% <<SECTION_3_BEGIN>>
The baseline does not cover untested compilers, devices, limiter choices, internal time-step stages, or MHD. Report 2 should add an Orszag--Tang MHD validation entry with Dedner-type divergence cleaning on the same matched-binary CPU/GPU framework. The tested Euler baseline fixes what must be preserved before the MHD extension is judged.
