\chapter{Validation and Precision Results}

\section{Validation Overview}
% <<SECTION_1_BEGIN>>
Chapter~5 interprets the Chapter~4 design matrix outputs: one- and
two-dimensional Euler validation, direct fp32--fp64 differences, matched
strict-IEEE HLLC CPU/GPU saved-output comparisons, and selected method or toolchain
sensitivity probes. Precision claims are pairwise differences or
reference-scaled ratios; CPU/GPU claims are restricted to saved conservative
states. A periodic-boundary audit using the same finite-volume update path gave
max relative drift \(\le 7.2\times10^{-14}\), checking telescoping conservation
rather than benchmark accuracy.
% <<SECTION_1_END>>

\section{One-Dimensional Euler Validation}
% <<SECTION_2_BEGIN>>
Before any fp32--fp64 or CPU--GPU gap is read, the MUSCL--Hancock--HLLC path
must reproduce the expected Riemann structures of \citet{toro2009}. Sod
provides the standard rarefaction--contact--shock check of
\citet{sod_1978}. In Fig.~\ref{fig:ch5-sod-comparison} density, velocity, and
pressure follow the exact solution, with the feature-scale comparison
quantified in Table~\ref{tab:ch5-1d-feature-validation}.

Toro3 and Toro5 stress the same code path with stronger waves. Toro3 contains a
right-running supersonic shock, while Toro5 involves colliding supersonic
shocks. Figures~\ref{fig:ch5-toro3-comparison}
and~\ref{fig:ch5-toro5-comparison} show the expected wave ordering with
grid-scale smearing at sharp waves.

The numerical summaries support the same interpretation. At $N=200$, the
conservative-state fp64--fp32 $L_1$ difference spans $\sim 10^{-8}$ to
$\sim 10^{-4}$ across the three cases. The matching reference-scaled density
ratios at $N=800$ sit below $10^{-4}$ (Table~\ref{tab:ch5-1d-summary}). These
are pairwise precision differences, not separate exact-solution errors. The
matched CPU/GPU HLLC comparisons give $L_1=0$, $L_\infty=0$, and
$\mathrm{ULP}_{\max}=0$ for Toro3 and Toro5 in both precisions at final time.
The later hardware discussion therefore starts from zero-drift saved
one-dimensional final states for these cases.

Table~\ref{tab:ch5-1d-exact-convergence} gives the exact density errors behind
this scale. The observed orders are below two, as expected for shock-tube
solutions whose global $L_1$ error is dominated by discontinuity neighbourhoods.
In smooth regions MUSCL--Hancock can recover higher-order behaviour, but a shock
or contact leaves an $O(1)$ jump smeared over $O(\Delta x)$ cells, so the
domain-integrated error retains a first-order component. The band split in
Table~\ref{tab:ch5-1d-feature-validation} is consistent with this: narrow
discontinuity bands contribute much larger density error than the remaining
cells. The observed $L_1$ orders of $0.65$--$0.79$ in 1D and $\approx 0.47$ for LW3 line up with the near-first-order $L_1$ convergence reported for MUSCL-type schemes on shock-containing problems~\citep{eccomas_2016_muscl, toro2009, leveque_2002}.

\section{Two-Dimensional Euler Validation}
% <<SECTION_3_BEGIN>>
The two-dimensional tests extend the validation from isolated shock tubes to
interacting Riemann structures. Liska-Wendroff configuration 3 (LW3) is the
four-shock case from \citet{liska_wendroff_2003}, with supersonic shock
segments on all four quadrant interfaces. In the $N=400$ fp64 schlieren field,
Fig.~\ref{fig:ch5-lw3-schlieren} shows a compact diamond-like shock interaction
around the centre, with curved contact-like structures inside the shocked
region. Against the $1600^2$ fp64 numerical reference, the fp64 density $L_1$ error
decreases and SSIM increases between $N=200$ and $N=400$
(Table~\ref{tab:ch5-2d-summary}). A self-convergence check gives $\rho$ $L_1$
decreasing from $3.304289{\times}10^{-3}$ for 400-vs-800 to
$2.392109{\times}10^{-3}$ for 800-vs-1600, with observed order about $0.47$;
this supports a numerical-reference hierarchy, not an exact-solution proof.
At $N=400$, the same-resolution fp32--fp64 density $L_1$ perturbation is
$9.25{\times}10^{-5}$ of the same-grid density reference error and
$1.39{\times}10^{-4}$ of the adjacent 400-vs-800 fp64 hierarchy error. The
precision result is therefore a perturbation-scale claim, not an assertion that
the $1600^2$ field is exact.

Liska-Wendroff configuration 12 (LW12) is treated as a separate
two-dimensional Riemann problem, not as a repeat of LW3. Its initial data
contain two supersonic shock segments and two contact interfaces
\citep{liska_wendroff_2003}. Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-lw12-schlieren} shows a
different topology: a large curved shock arc reaches towards the upper-right
corner, with a second curved front and a small shear/contact roll-up near the
central interaction. The reference comparison uses a higher-resolution $800^2$
fp64 numerical reference, not an exact solution. With that reference, the fp64
density $L_1$ error drops and SSIM rises between $N=200$ and $N=400$
(Table~\ref{tab:ch5-2d-summary}). The fp32--fp64 density gap at $N=400$ sits
below the available reference scale, but this ratio is not a convergence proof
for the $800^2$ reference.

\section{Single- and Double-Precision Comparison}
% <<SECTION_4_BEGIN>>
Using the reference-scaled ratio defined in Equation~\ref{eq:ch4-rref},
Fig.~\ref{fig:ch5-float-double-over-reference} reports the direct IEEE
fp32--fp64 density comparison for LW3. For density, $R_\rho=4.47\times10^{-5}$ at
$N=200$ and $R_\rho=9.25\times10^{-5}$ at $N=400$. The ratio therefore
remains far below one, but it grows with resolution because the reference error
shrinks faster than the fp32--fp64 drift. The same pattern appears in the
second two-dimensional case: for LW12 against the $800^2$ fp64 reference,
$R_\rho=4.63\times10^{-5}$ at $N=200$ and
$R_\rho=1.30\times10^{-4}$ at $N=400$.

The one-dimensional exact-reference comparisons give a similar scale for the selected
shock-tube tests. At $N=800$, the density fp32--fp64 gap divided by the
fp64--exact error is $1.06\times10^{-4}$ for Sod,
$1.36\times10^{-5}$ for Toro3, and $3.91\times10^{-5}$ for Toro5.
The stationary-contact pressure ratio is reported as $\infty$ because the
fp64--exact denominator is zero; this case is a degenerate-reference result,
not a quantitative precision-adequacy ratio.

These measurements show that, for the tested Euler cases, the final-time direct
fp32--fp64 density and pressure differences sit below the available
reference scale~\citep{higham_2002}. The growing two-dimensional ratios warn
that the comparison may shift with the reference. The
localised LW12 density difference
in Fig.~\ref{fig:ch5-lw12-precision-heatmap} is consistent with precision
sensitivity in the steep pressure and density gradients of the upper-right
shock-interaction region. Those gradients drive extrema in local wave-speed
estimates, which enter the HLLC star-state and flux algebra of Chapter~3. The
figure does not prove that a specific HLLC branch changed; no branch-count
diagnostic is used here. Virtual-precision sensitivity maps appear in
Chapter~6.

\section{Matched CPU/GPU Comparison}
% <<SECTION_5_BEGIN>>
The matched CPU/GPU comparison asks a narrower question than the fp32--fp64
study: for a fixed case, precision, solver, and binary, does changing the
runtime device alter the saved state? Table~\ref{tab:ch5-cpu-gpu}
summarises the coverage without listing each zero-valued comparison
separately. All entries use \texttt{solver=hllc} and \texttt{STRICT\_IEEE=ON};
the strict build disables fast-math and fused-operation shortcuts that would
otherwise make a device comparison less controlled. Where raw grids were
available, byte-level and ULP metrics were used; the zero-drift table includes
metrics-backed saved-state comparisons, with any LW3 fp64 final raw-grid gap
handled as metrics-only.

All 14 final-output comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:ch5-cpu-gpu} are zero in
\(L_1\), \(L_\infty\), and \(\mathrm{ULP}_{\max}\), as are the
saved-checkpoint comparisons listed in the table. Three matched-binary
properties make this absence of saved-state divergence expected:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item saved conservative states come from matched strict-IEEE builds, with
        fast-math reassociation, FMA contraction, denormal flush-to-zero, and
        approximate division and square root disabled on both backends
        (Table~\ref{tab:ch4-strict-flags});
  \item the write-out path has no floating-point summation reduction, so saved
        bytes follow the same ordered local operations on either backend;
  \item global CFL selection in Equation~\ref{eq:ch4-cfl-scan} is an ordered
        \(\max/\min\) over cell-local wave-speed candidates, not an unordered
        sum, so \(\Delta t\) is independent of block, warp, or thread order.
\end{enumerate}
The GPU is nonetheless doing live arithmetic, not echoing CPU outputs. The
CUDA flag probe below shows that switching from strict to fast-math CUDA
controls perturbs the same saved states by
\(\mathrm{ULP}_{\max}\approx 30.7\) on LW3 fp32. An inert GPU path could not
produce that perturbation. The zero-drift result is therefore a statement
about what the matched strict build hides, not about whether the GPU is
exercised.

\section{Toro Test 2 Branch Stability}
% <<SECTION_7_BEGIN>>
Toro Test 2 is the symmetric near-vacuum problem. With the same CPU--fp64
toolchain and CFL, the baseline $\leq$ branch finishes it in roughly
$0.13$~s, while the strict $<$ branch produces no valid final state. The
first-step negative-density failure is analysed below. The branches differ
only in one HLLC-dispatcher comparison.

The mechanism is analytic. Toro Test 2 has $u_L = -2$, $u_R = +2$, $\rho_L = \rho_R = \rho$ and $p_L = p_R$, hence $a_L = a_R = a$. Substituting into Eq.~\ref{eq:ch3-sstar-decomp}, the pressure jump $p_R - p_L$ vanishes and the momentum-wave terms cancel pairwise, $\rho_L u_L (S_L - u_L) - \rho_R u_R (S_R - u_R) = 2\rho a - 2\rho a = 0$, so $N_\ast = 0$ exactly by symmetry. The denominator is $D_\ast = -2\rho a$, well-conditioned and bounded away from zero. Analytically $S_\ast = 0$ from Eq.~\ref{eq:ch3-hllc-sstar}, but the computed $N_\ast$ carries rounding noise, so the sign of the computed $S_\ast$ is set by that noise.

A stage-resolved diagnostic logs the trigger directly. At the central interface on step 0 it records $N_\ast = 0$, $D_\ast=-1.496663$ and $S_\ast=-0$. The baseline $\leq$ rule selects $F_{\ast L}$ and gives zero mass flux, but strict $<$ excludes both star inequalities at equality and falls through to $F_R$, giving mass flux $2.0$. After that first update the state dump reports $\min\rho=-0.164343$ at cell 99. The missing final \texttt{grid.bin} is therefore a downstream symptom of an immediate non-physical update, not merely timeout evidence. The conclusion remains single-case, but the failure trigger is logged.
